## Background: the oldest large corpus of Egyptian religious literature

The Pyramid Texts are the oldest and largest surviving body of Egyptian religious literature. They first appear carved on the interior walls of the pyramid of King Unas, at the end of the 5th Dynasty (roughly the 24th century BCE), and continue to be inscribed in pyramids throughout the 6th Dynasty and beyond. Individual spells are called "utterances," and their purpose is to protect and empower the deceased king on his journey to the afterlife and to sustain him there as an eternal being.

The Pyramid Texts represent the earliest attested stage of the Egyptian language, Old Egyptian, even though many of their spells were subsequently carried forward — and adapted and expanded — into the Middle Kingdom's Coffin Texts (see [The Coffin Texts: An Excerpt](/en/old-egyptian/readings/coffin-texts-excerpt/)).

## Example: a phrase for ascension to the sky

The phrase below is a short illustrative example reflecting a theme common throughout the Pyramid Texts — the deceased king's ascent to the sky and attainment of eternal life — not a verbatim quotation from any specific utterance.

<p class="hieroglyph">𓂝𓈖𓐍 𓅓 𓊪𓏏</p>

```
ꜥnḫ      m      pt
life     in     sky
```

*ꜥnḫ m pt*, "life in the sky," is an illustrative phrase. Many Pyramid Texts utterances repeatedly depict the deceased king ascending to the sky and attaining eternal life (ꜥnḫ) alongside the gods, and this short phrase condenses that central image.
