## An Edict Issued in an Age of Turmoil

The **Proclamation of Telepinu** is a document issued by Telepinu, who took the throne toward the end of the Hittite Old Kingdom (around the 16th century BCE), and is best known for establishing clear rules of succession in order to end an era of repeated assassinations and internal strife over the throne. The first half of the text is a historical preamble looking back on the reigns of the first king, Labarna, and Ḫattušili I, as an ideal age; the second half then lays out concrete rules drawn from those lessons.

## The Rules of Royal Succession

At the heart of the proclamation are rules determining who should become the next king.

Below is a simplified example sentence, for learning purposes, conveying the gist of these rules (note that this is not a verbatim quotation of a specific line, but a summary rendering of the content of the provision).

```
DUMU.NÍTA-aš 1-aš LUGAL-uš ki-ša-ru
son-NOM first-NOM king-NOM become-IMP.3SG.MP
"let the first (-ranked) son become king"

ták-ku DUMU.NÍTA-aš 1-aš NU.GÁL
if son first not.exist
"if there is no first (-ranked) son"

nu-za ku-iš DUMU.NÍTA-aš 2-aš LUGAL-uš ki-ša-ru
and-(reflexive) whichever son second-of king-NOM become-IMP.3SG
"let whichever son of the second rank become king"
```

## Judgment by the "Panku"

Another key pillar of Telepinu's proclamation is an assembly/community institution called the **panku**. The edict rules that if bloodshed occurs within the royal family, the punishment of the offender should not be a private act of revenge by the victim's kin, but should instead be deliberated and decided by the panku — a council made up of the royal family and high officials. This is regarded as a progressive administrative innovation for its time, aimed at breaking the chain of assassinations over royal succession.

## Historical Significance

Telepinu's proclamation is valued not merely as a legal document but also as a precious historical narrative preserving the early history of the Hittite Kingdom, including the deeds of Labarna, Ḫattušili I, and Muršili I. The clear codification of succession rules and the establishment of judgment through the panku are thought to have had a lasting influence on the political system of the Hittite Kingdom thereafter.
