## Adjectives follow the noun

Old Egyptian adjectives agree with the noun they modify in gender and number, and are placed **after** the noun. This is the reverse of English word order ("great god") and closer to Romance languages such as French (*dieu grand*).

<p class="hieroglyph">𓈖𓍿𓂋𓂝𓄿</p>

```
nṯr ꜥꜣ
god great
"the great god" (literally: god great)
```

Noun-adjective order is the default pattern to remember first. Adjectives also agree in gender and number with the noun (see [Noun Gender and Number](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/noun-gender-number/)), so a feminine or plural noun triggers the corresponding -t or -w/-wt ending on the adjective as well.

## Nisbe adjectives: deriving adjectives from nouns with -y

Egyptian has a productive process for deriving adjectives meaning "of / belonging to / pertaining to X" by adding the suffix **-y** to a noun — often a place name or a common noun. This class is called the **nisbe adjective**, a term borrowed from Arabic grammar's *nisbah* (a parallel process that adds -ī to a noun to form a relative adjective). It is a striking typological parallel between two languages of the same Afroasiatic family.

For instance, adding -y to **rmṯ** "people" yields an adjectival form meaning roughly "of the people, human" (this is presented here as an illustration of how productive the -y suffix is, not as a specific citation to memorize word-for-word).

<p class="hieroglyph">𓂋𓅓𓍿</p>

```
rmṯ
"people, mankind"
```

<p class="hieroglyph">𓂋𓅓𓍿𓇌</p>

```
rmṯy
rmṯ (people) + -y (nisbe suffix)
"of the people, human" (illustrating the -y suffix pattern)
```

## Nisbes also function as nouns

A key further feature of nisbe adjectives is that they can stand alone as **nouns**. Nisbes derived from place names in particular become nouns denoting "a person from X" — designating origin, ethnicity, or occupation — and the same -y pattern is used to coin professional titles. Nisbe formation thus straddles adjectival and nominal use, making it one of the most productive word-formation devices in Egyptian.
