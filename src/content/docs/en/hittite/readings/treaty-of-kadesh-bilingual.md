## From the Battle of Kadesh to Peace

The **Treaty of Kadesh** was concluded around 1259 BCE between the Hittite king Ḫattušili III and the Egyptian pharaoh Ramesses II. Roughly fifteen years earlier, the two powers had fought a major engagement near the Syrian city of Kadesh — the Battle of Kadesh — which ended inconclusively, leaving both sides locked in a long stalemate without a decisive advantage. To break this impasse, the two states eventually concluded a peace treaty.

## The Oldest Surviving "Two-Sided" International Treaty

What makes this treaty especially significant in world history is the extremely rare fact that **copies survive from both signatory parties, each in its own language**. The Egyptian copy is carved in hieroglyphs on temple walls at sites such as Karnak, while the Hittite copy survives as clay tablets in the archives at Ḫattuša, written in cuneiform (in Akkadian, the diplomatic lingua franca of the day, with a Hittite-language version also known). Comparing the two versions has confirmed that their content largely matches, making this a first-rate source for the study of ancient international treaties.

## Main Provisions of the Treaty

Unlike the vassal treaties discussed in [Treaty Formulas](/en/hittite/readings/kbo-treaty-formula/), this treaty is distinctive as an agreement between two peer great powers. Its main provisions include:

- A declaration of permanent peace and friendship between the two states
- A mutual non-aggression pledge
- Mutual military assistance if either party is attacked by a third state
- Mutual extradition of political fugitives, with a guarantee of lenient treatment for them
- An oath sworn with the gods of both lands ("the thousand gods of Egypt," "the thousand gods of Hatti") as witnesses

## A Formulaic Oath

Below is a simplified example sentence, for learning purposes, conveying the gist of the clause swearing lasting friendship between the two states.

```
nu-za KUR URUḪa-at-ti KUR URUMi-iz-ri-ya-qa
(reflexive) land Ḫatti land Egypt-and
"the land of Ḫatti and the land of Egypt"

a-ra-a-u-e-eš-ša-an ḫa-an-te-ez-zi-ya-za za-aḫ-ḫi-ya-za
forever from.the.outset battle-from(negated)
"shall cease from fighting forever, from now on"
```

## Symbolic Significance Today

Given its historical importance, the Treaty of Kadesh is often referred to today as the "oldest surviving international peace treaty." A replica of the treaty is displayed at the United Nations headquarters in New York, and it is sometimes cited symbolically in modern international relations as an ancient precedent for the peaceful resolution of disputes between states.
