Sanskrit numerals decline for gender and case like nouns and adjectives, but their patterns vary considerably depending on the number itself. This article surveys cardinal numbers 1 through 10, examining how (or whether) each declines, before turning to the formation of ordinal numbers.

## Cardinal Numbers 1-10

| Number | Word | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | एक eka | Singular only. Partly takes pronominal endings (the tad type) |
| 2 | द्वि dvi | Dual only. Little variation by gender |
| 3 | त्रि tri | Plural only. Sharply different forms by gender |
| 4 | चतुर् catur | Plural only. Sharply different forms by gender |
| 5 | पञ्च pañca | Plural only. No gender variation (invariant) |
| 6 | षष् ṣaṣ | Plural only. No gender variation |
| 7 | सप्त sapta | Plural only. No gender variation |
| 8 | अष्ट aṣṭa | Plural only. No gender variation |
| 9 | नव nava | Plural only. No gender variation |
| 10 | दश daśa | Plural only. No gender variation |

Numbers 5 through 10 share a common trait: although they decline for case, they show no gender variation — the same form is used regardless of whether they modify a masculine, feminine, or neuter noun.

## Eka "One": Singular Only, Partly Pronominal

Since eka "one" necessarily counts a singular referent, it is naturally used only in the singular. Its declension is close to that of an a-stem noun (masculine eka-, neuter eka-) and ā-stem (feminine ekā-), but some of its dative, ablative, and locative forms use the **pronominal endings** (-smai, -smāt, -smin, and so on) seen with tasmai, tasmāt, and tasmin in [Pronoun Declension](/en/sanskrit/grammar/pronoun-declension/).

```
एकः पुरुषः एकं ग्रामं गच्छति।
ekaḥ puruṣaḥ ekaṃ grāmaṃ gacchati.
one-NOM(M) man-NOM one-ACC(N) village-ACC go-3SG.PRES
"One man goes to one village."
```

## Dvi "Two": Dual Only

Dvi "two" is inherently dual in meaning and declines only for the dual. It takes the form dvau in the masculine nominative/vocative/accusative, and dve in the feminine and neuter.

```
द्वौ बालकौ क्रीडतः।
dvau bālakau krīḍataḥ.
two-NOM(M.DU) boy-NOM.DU play-3DU.PRES
"Two boys play."
```

## Tri "Three" and Catur "Four": Plural Only, Sharply Different by Gender

Tri "three" and catur "four" decline only for the plural but, unlike eka and dvi, show a striking feature: **the stem itself changes dramatically depending on gender**.

| Number | Masc. nominative plural | Fem. nominative plural | Neut. nominative plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | त्रयः trayaḥ | तिस्रः tisraḥ | त्रीणि trīṇi |
| 4 | चत्वारः catvāraḥ | चतस्रः catasraḥ | चत्वारि catvāri |

The feminine forms (tisraḥ, catasraḥ) using an entirely distinct stem from the masculine and neuter forms is a phenomenon specific to the numbers three and four among Sanskrit numerals.

```
तिस्रः कन्याः गायन्ति।
tisraḥ kanyāḥ gāyanti.
three-NOM(F) girl.PL-NOM sing-3PL.PRES
"Three girls sing."
```

## Numbers Above 10

The numbers 11 through 19 (एकादश ekādaśa "11," etc.) take their own compound-like forms, while the decade numbers 20 (विंशति viṃśati), 30 (त्रिंशत् triṃśat), and so on are treated as feminine nouns of the ī-stem or ti-stem class. 100 (शत śata) and 1000 (सहस्र sahasra) decline as ordinary neuter nouns. As numbers grow larger, the numeral behaves increasingly like an ordinary noun, tending to combine with the noun it counts via the genitive rather than in simple apposition (for example, "a hundred men" can take a structure closer to "a hundred of men").

## Ordinal Numbers

Ordinal numbers ("the Nth") are formed from the cardinals by regular or semi-regular suffixes, and decline like ordinary adjectives — a-stem (masculine/neuter), ā-stem (feminine).

| Cardinal | Ordinal (masculine form) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | प्रथम prathama- | first |
| 2 | द्वितीय dvitīya- | second |
| 3 | तृतीय tṛtīya- | third |
| 4 | चतुर्थ caturtha- | fourth |
| 5 | पञ्चम pañcama- | fifth |
| 6 | षष्ठ ṣaṣṭha- | sixth |
| 7 | सप्तम saptama- | seventh |
| 8 | अष्टम aṣṭama- | eighth |
| 9 | नवम navama- | ninth |
| 10 | दशम daśama- | tenth |

```
सः प्रथमः पुत्रः।
saḥ prathamaḥ putraḥ.
he-NOM first-NOM(M) son-NOM
"He is the first son."
```

Note that the ordinals corresponding to "1," "2," and "3" — prathama-, dvitīya-, tṛtīya- — do not share a root with the cardinals eka, dvi, tri (a phenomenon comparable to English first, second, third not sharing a root with one, two, three).
