## Signs that record quantity directly, not words

[Numbers and Counting](/en/sumerian/readings/numbers-and-counting/) covered Sumerian's sexagesimal system and the spoken number words (`diš` "one," `min` "two," and so on). Separate from those number words, cuneiform also has dedicated numeral signs that record a quantity directly — and these are what administrative and economic texts overwhelmingly use to record amounts. This article looks at the actual glyphs involved.

## 1-9: incised digit signs

In later tablets (especially from the Old Babylonian period onward), the numbers 1 through 9 are each usually written with a dedicated incised-digit sign (a single symbol bundling several wedges together).

| Number | Glyph | Corresponding sign name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 𒁹 | DIŠ |
| 3 | 𒐈 | 3(DIŠ) |
| 4 | 𒐉 | 4(DIŠ) |
| 5 | 𒐊 | 5(DIŠ) |
| 6 | 𒐋 | 6(DIŠ) |
| 7 | 𒐌 | 7(DIŠ) |
| 8 | 𒐍 | 8(DIŠ) |
| 9 | 𒐎 | 9(DIŠ) |

## Multiples of ten: dedicated signs

10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 likewise each have their own dedicated glyph.

| Number | Glyph | Corresponding sign name |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 𒌋 | U |
| 20 | 𒎙 | U.U |
| 30 | 𒌍 | U.U.U |
| 40 | 𒐏 | 4(U) |
| 50 | 𒐐 | 5(U) |

## The older system: the same shape, distinguished by size

Before these incised digit signs became standard, an older system — descended from the original clay accounting tokens — indicated place value purely through the *size* of a dedicated impression. As introduced in [Numbers and Counting](/en/sumerian/readings/numbers-and-counting/), this older system worked as follows.

| Number | Glyph (sign name) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 𒁹 (DIŠ) | a small impression |
| 60 | 𒁹 (DIŠ, large) | the same shape as 1, but a larger impression |
| 10 | 𒌋 (U) | a small impression |
| 600 | 𒌋 (U, large) | the same shape as 10, but a larger impression |
| 3,600 (60×60) | 𒊹 (ŠAR2) | an even larger, dedicated circular sign |

It's a striking design choice: rather than inventing an entirely new sign for each higher power of 60, the system reused existing shapes at a different size — an efficient piece of engineering.
