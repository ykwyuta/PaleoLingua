## The acrophonic principle

A defining feature of the runic alphabet is that every letter has a name, and the first sound of that name matches the letter's sound value. This is called the **acrophonic principle**. The rune ᚠ, for example, is named *fé* ("wealth," "livestock"), and its initial sound /f/ is the rune's own reading. Likewise ᛏ is *týr* (the name of the war-god Týr), giving initial /t/, and ᛒ is *bjarkan* ("birch"), giving initial /b/.

The idea is similar to how the Latin letter "A" ultimately derives from a Phoenician letter meaning "ox," or to phonetic borrowing in Egyptian hieroglyphs — but runes are distinctive in that the names remained live, meaningful vocabulary within the spoken language itself. Because of this, runes were treated not merely as sound symbols but as characters carrying an "ideographic afterglow" of the concept their name pointed to — wealth, giant, sun, ice, and so on.

## Rune poems

Medieval tradition preserves several verse texts — **rune poems** — that gloss each rune's name with its symbolic meaning. The three best known are the *Old Icelandic Rune Poem*, the *Old Norwegian Rune Poem*, and the *Old English Rune Poem*, each assigning a short verse to every letter. The Old Icelandic Rune Poem, for instance, describes ᚢ (*úr*) this way:

```
úr er skýja grátr
ok skára þverrir
ok hirðis hatr.
umbre visir.
```

**Gloss**: "*úr* (rain) is the weeping of clouds, and the destroyer of the ripened crop, and the hatred of the shepherd. 〈A sign of rain.〉"

Rune poems like this link each letter's name to proverb-like images drawn from nature and daily life.

## Semantic fields in the sixteen names

Grouping the sixteen Younger Futhark names by semantic field gives a glimpse of the Viking Age worldview behind them.

| Field | Runes and names |
|---|---|
| Nature and weather | ᚢ úr ("rain"/"aurochs"), ᚼ hagall ("hail"), ᛁ ís ("ice"), ᛅ ár ("good harvest"), ᛋ sól ("sun") |
| Mythological beings | ᚦ þurs ("giant"), ᚬ áss ("god," esp. of the Æsir), ᛏ týr (the war-god Týr) |
| People and society | ᚠ fé ("wealth," "livestock"), ᛘ maðr ("man"), ᚱ reið ("riding," "journey") |
| Plants | ᛒ bjarkan ("birch"), ᛦ ýr ("yew") |
| Abstract concepts | ᚾ nauð ("need," "hardship"), ᛚ lögr ("water"; sometimes connected etymologically to "law") |

## Summary: names and sound values

| Rune | Name | Meaning | Sound value(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ᚠ | fé | wealth, livestock | f |
| ᚢ | úr | rain, aurochs | u, o, y, ø, w |
| ᚦ | þurs | giant | þ, ð |
| ᚬ | áss | god (of the Æsir) | a, æ, o |
| ᚱ | reið | riding | r |
| ᚴ | kaun | boil, sore | k, g |
| ᚼ | hagall | hail | h |
| ᚾ | nauð | need, hardship | n |
| ᛁ | ís | ice | i, e |
| ᛅ | ár | good harvest | a |
| ᛋ | sól | sun | s |
| ᛏ | týr | the war-god Týr | t, d |
| ᛒ | bjarkan | birch | b, p |
| ᛘ | maðr | man | m |
| ᛚ | lögr | water | l |
| ᛦ | ýr | yew | y, R |
