## Another way to express possession

[The Genitive -ak](/en/sumerian/grammar/genitive-case-ak/) showed how "noun + noun + `-ak`" expresses a possessive relationship. Separately, Sumerian also has possessive pronominal suffixes corresponding to the personal pronouns, which attach directly to a noun to build expressions like "my ~" or "your ~."

## The basic paradigm

Using `e2` (house) as our example noun:

| Person | Suffix | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st singular | `-ĝu10` | e2-ĝu10 | my house |
| 2nd singular | `-zu` | e2-zu | your house |
| 3rd singular (animate) | `-a-ni` | e2-a-ni | his/her house |
| 3rd singular (inanimate/anaphoric) | `-bi` | e2-bi | its house, that house |

```
𒂍𒈬
e2-ĝu10
e2-ĝu10
house-my
"my house"
```

```
𒂍𒀀𒉌
e2-a-ni
e2-a-ni
house-his/her
"his/her house"
```

## Why the 3rd person singular splits into two forms

The 3rd person singular possessive suffix takes two different shapes: `-a-ni` (for an animate, personal possessor) and `-bi` (for an inanimate possessor, or anaphoric reference to something already mentioned). `-bi` is the same form introduced in [Pronouns: Personal and Demonstrative](/en/sumerian/grammar/personal-demonstrative-pronouns/) as the demonstrative/anaphoric word for "that/it" — reflecting a broader Sumerian pattern of treating an inanimate possessor not as a "person" but as a "thing" referred to demonstratively.

## Relationship to verbal person affixes

These possessive suffixes belong to a different system from the verbal person affixes covered in [Person Affixes and the Basic Verb Structure](/en/sumerian/grammar/personal-affixes-verb-basics/). The suffixes attached to nouns and those attached to verbs take different shapes, so take care not to conflate the two systems.
