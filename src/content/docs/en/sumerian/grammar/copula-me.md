## Building a nominal sentence: the suffix -am3

The most basic way to build an "A is B" sentence (a nominal predicate sentence) in Sumerian is to attach the suffix `-am3` directly after the predicate noun.

<p class="cuneiform">𒌨𒀭𒇉   𒈗𒀀𒀭</p>

```
ur-dnamma   lugal-am3
Ur-Namma    king-COP
"Ur-Namma is king."
```

`-am3` builds a complete nominal-predicate sentence without any of the complex person-marking machinery a verb requires. It is especially common in third-person sentences ("he/she/it is ___").

## First- and second-person nominal sentences

For first and second person, the `-men`-type forms already seen in [Pronouns](/en/sumerian/grammar/personal-demonstrative-pronouns/) are used instead (e.g., `-me-en`, "I am ___").

<p class="cuneiform">𒂷𒂊   𒈗𒈨𒂗</p>

```
ĝe26-e   lugal-me-en
I        king-COP-1SG
"I am king."
```

## The verb me: existential sentences

While `-am3` is specifically a suffix for nominal predicate sentences ("A is B"), the independent verb `me` ("to be," "to exist") is used in a broader range of contexts — for instance, linking multiple predicates together, or making a purely existential statement ("there is/are").

## Summary

| Use | Form | Cuneiform |
|---|---|---|
| 3rd-person nominal predicate | noun + `-am3` | 𒀀𒀭 |
| 1st-person nominal predicate | noun + `-me-en` | 𒈨𒂗 |
| Independent verb "to be" | `me` | 𒈨 |
