## Connectives as the "Platform" Linking Sentences

As discussed in [The Enclitic Pronoun Chain](/en/hittite/grammar/enclitic-pronoun-chain/), Hittite sentences often open with a clause-initial connective that functions as a "platform" anchoring the following string of enclitics to the front of the clause. The three main ones are **nu**, **ta**, and **šu**. All three are connective particles that loosely link the preceding context to the following clause, with a meaning close to "and."

## Distinguishing the Three Connectives

| Connective | Rough meaning | Tendency of use |
|---|---|---|
| nu | "and," "so" | Used broadly across all periods, becoming increasingly dominant in later (New Hittite) texts |
| ta | "and," "then" | Especially favored in Old Hittite narrative texts |
| šu | "and" | Relatively rare, restricted to particular contexts and periods |

All three connectives are close in meaning to "and," and it is difficult to pin down a single, strict semantic distinction among them. Rather, which connective is chosen is thought to depend heavily on **style, genre, and the period of composition**.

## A Concrete Example

```
nu-za URU-an ḫar-ni-ik-ta
and-(reflexive) city-ACC destroy-3SG.PRET
"and he destroyed the city"

ta LUGAL-uš pa-a-it
and king-NOM go-3SG.PRET
"and the king went"
```

In both examples, the clause-initial connective (nu, ta) introduces what follows — the reflexive particle -za, or the subject LUGAL-uš "the king" — functioning as the syntactic starting point of the whole clause.

## Change Over Time

In Old Hittite narrative texts (such as the Anitta Text), ta is frequently used, while in New Hittite documents nu becomes established as the nearly standard connective, with the use of ta declining relatively. šu is not especially frequent at any stage and tends to appear in particular contexts. Shifts in how frequently these connectives are used serve as one clue for dating a text, alongside the [chronological stages](/en/hittite/script-and-phonology/what-is-hittite/) of the language.
