## The proverb-collection genre

Sumerian has a genre of short sayings and maxims called `inim-gar` ("proverb, saying"), gathered into "proverb collections." Most of what survives today was copied out in Old Babylonian-period (roughly the 19th-18th centuries BCE) scribal schools (*edubba*, `e2-dub-ba`, "tablet house") as writing-practice exercises. Manuscripts of more than twenty distinct proverb collections have been excavated at sites such as Nippur and Ur, covering a wide range of themes: animal fables, the subtleties of human relationships, and wry commentary on labor and social status.

## Example: a saying starring an animal

Proverb collections contain many sayings that use an animal as the protagonist to satirize human behavior. The example below reproduces the genre's typical style as a learning exercise — it is an illustrative reconstruction of the genre's typical phrasing, not a word-for-word quotation of a specific entry from a specific collection.

```
𒌨𒂊       𒅗𒃻       𒉡𒍪
ur-e       inim       nu-zu
ur-e       inim       nu-zu
dog-ERG    word       NEG-know
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Word | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ur-e | ur (dog) + -e (ergative) | the dog |
| inim | noun | word |
| nu-zu | nu- (negative) + zu (to know) | does not know |

**Japanese**: 「犬は言葉を知らない。」
**English**: "A dog does not know words."

## What makes proverbs fun to read

A line like this may look, on its surface, like a simple observation, but such animal-protagonist sayings are usually read as an oblique satire aimed at someone in human society (the ignorant, the presumptuous, and so on). Because proverb collections are built from a series of short sentences, they're also an excellent way to review the basic grammar covered in earlier articles, such as [The Negative Prefix nu-](/en/sumerian/grammar/negation-nu/) and [Ergative and Absolutive](/en/sumerian/grammar/sumerian-ergative/).

## Vocabulary and structural notes

- The ergative `-e` in `ur-e` ("the dog") is the marker of a transitive verb's agent, covered in [Ergative and Absolutive in Sumerian](/en/sumerian/grammar/sumerian-ergative/).
- `nu-zu` ("does not know") shows the negative prefix from [The Negative Prefix nu-](/en/sumerian/grammar/negation-nu/) attaching directly to a verb root.
- Because proverb collections tend to use grammatically short, simple sentences, they're well suited to letting beginners repeatedly encounter familiar grammar across a variety of new vocabulary.
