## The definite article attaches to the noun

The equivalent of English "the" appears in Old Norse not as a separate word but as a **suffix fused onto the noun's ending** — a defining feature of the North Germanic languages (shared with modern Icelandic, Faroese, and Norwegian) that has no counterpart in West Germanic languages like English or German.

## Origin: the demonstrative pronoun hinn

The suffix descends from an originally independent demonstrative pronoun, *hinn* ("that," "the"). It had forms *hinn* (masculine), *hin* (feminine), and *hit* (neuter) depending on gender, number, and case; as it fused onto the preceding noun, the initial *h* was lost, leaving the suffixes *-inn, -in, -it*.

## Declension pattern: maðr ("man")

Let's look at the strong masculine noun *maðr* ("man") with the definite article attached.

| Case | No article (sg.) | With article (sg., "the man") |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | maðr | maðrinn |
| Accusative | mann | manninn |
| Genitive | manns | mannsins |
| Dative | manni | manninum |

The plural takes the suffix the same way.

| Case | No article (pl.) | With article (pl., "the men") |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | menn | mennirnir |
| Accusative | menn | mennina |
| Genitive | manna | mannanna |
| Dative | mönnum | mönnunum |

Feminine and neuter nouns take their own gender-appropriate suffix (feminine *-in*, neuter *-it*). The feminine noun *saga* ("story"), for instance, becomes *sagan* ("the story") in the nominative singular with the article attached.

## Example sentence

```
maðrinn  sér   hestinn
maðr-inn sé-r  hest-inn
man-the(NOM) see-3SG horse-the(ACC)
"The man sees the horse."
```

Where English needs a separate word "the" twice, Old Norse expresses the same meaning by fusing the article onto each noun.

## With an adjective: the free-standing hinn

When the noun is modified by an adjective, the standard construction uses the free-standing, prenominal *hinn/hin/hit* instead of the suffix (and the adjective takes the weak declension — see [Adjective Declension](/en/grammar/adjective-declension/)).

```
hinn gamli maðr
hinn  gaml-i  maðr
the   old-WEAK  man
"the old man"
```
