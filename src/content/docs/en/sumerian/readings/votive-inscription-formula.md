## The votive inscription genre

Sumerian kings and private individuals sometimes had a short inscription carved onto an object dedicated to a temple — a stone bowl, a statue, a model weapon — recording that the object had been offered to a particular deity. Like the [royal (building) inscriptions](/en/sumerian/readings/first-sentence-royal-inscription/), these "votive inscriptions" are highly formulaic, most repeating the basic pattern "PN (the dedicator) offered (this) to deity DN."

The example below is a simplified, illustrative composite drawn from wording common to multiple votive inscriptions — it is not a word-for-word quotation of any single inscription.

## Example

<p class="cuneiform">𒈗𒂊       𒀏𒊏      𒀀𒈬𒈾𒊒</p>

```
lugal-e    nanše-ra   a mu-na-ru
lugal-e    nanše-ra   a mu-na-ru
king-ERG   Nanše-DAT  he.dedicated.it.to.her
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Word | Cuneiform | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| lugal-e | 𒈗𒂊 | lugal (king) + -e (ergative) | the king |
| `d`nanše-ra | 𒀭𒀏𒊏 | proper noun (goddess) + -ra (dative) | to the goddess Nanše |
| a mu-na-ru | 𒀀𒈬𒈾𒊒 | a (offering, lit. "water") + mu-na- (ventive + dative person prefix) + ru (to offer) | (he) dedicated (it) to her |

**Japanese**: 「王がナンシェ女神に（これを）捧げた。」
**English**: "The king dedicated (this) to the goddess Nanše."

## About the formula a mu-na-ru

`a mu-na-ru` ("dedicated it") is an extremely common closing formula in votive inscriptions. `a` (literally "water") is thought to have broadened into an idiomatic term for the act of dedication itself; the verb `ru` ("to offer, to place") takes the ventive prefix `mu-` along with the dative person prefix `-na-` ("for her"), which cross-references the recipient.

## Vocabulary and structural notes

- As with `nanše-ra`, the recipient deity of a dedication takes the [dative -ra](/en/sumerian/grammar/dative-terminative-locative/) — the same idea seen in the "built (it) for ~" construction from [Reading Your First Sentence](/en/sumerian/readings/first-sentence-royal-inscription/).
- The sequence `mu-na-`, where the directional/voice prefix `mu-` is immediately followed by the person prefix `-na-`, is a concrete instance of the slot model introduced in [The Verbal Prefix Chain: A Structural Overview](/en/sumerian/grammar/verbal-prefix-chain-overview/).
- Votive inscriptions, alongside building inscriptions, are one of the best entry points for beginners to access primary sources through recognizable formulaic language.
