## Not "the year 2024," but "the year that ~ happened"

We typically count years with a running number (a calendar era). In Sumer — especially in the cities of the Ur III and Old Babylonian periods — years were instead widely identified by a "year name" (date formula, also called `mu-iti`), named for whatever particularly notable event occurred during that year. Administrative documents record, as their date, a short clause describing "what happened that year" — not "regnal year N of king X," but the event itself serving as the year's name.

## Example: a typical year-name pattern

The example below is a simplified, illustrative composite of wording common in year names commemorating a military conquest or campaign — not a word-for-word quotation of any specific year's actual name.

```
𒈬     𒀕𒆠      𒁀𒅆𒌨
mu     unugki    ba-hul
mu     unug{ki}  ba-hul
year   Uruk      it.was.destroyed
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Word | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| mu | noun | year |
| unugki | proper noun (place name) | Uruk (the city) |
| ba-hul | ba- (middle voice) + hul (to destroy) | it was destroyed |

**Japanese**: 「ウルクが破壊された年。」
**English**: "The year Uruk was destroyed."

## How the year-name system worked

Because scribes needed to know exactly which year name was currently in effect, administrators also compiled "year-name lists" cataloging them in order. Besides military events like the example above, year names could commemorate the construction of a temple, a princess's installation as a priestess, the digging of a canal, the manufacture of an important ritual object, and much more.

## Vocabulary and structural notes

- As with `unugki`, place names take the place-name determinative `ki` covered in [Logograms and Determinatives](/en/sumerian/script-and-phonology/logograms-and-determinatives/).
- The `ba-` in `ba-hul` is the middle-voice prefix from [The Ventive Prefix mu- and the Middle-Voice Prefix ba-/bi2-](/en/sumerian/grammar/ventive-and-middle-prefixes/), lending a nuance of describing the event from a viewpoint somewhat removed from the speaker.
- Year names also appear frequently as the date at the end of the economic documents covered in [Reading an Ur III Administrative Tablet](/en/sumerian/readings/ur-iii-administrative-tablet/) — reading both articles together makes real documents considerably easier to follow.
