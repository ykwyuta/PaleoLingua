## The role of prepositions in Egyptian

Old Egyptian has no case inflection, so much of the relationship between nouns, or between a noun and a verb, is carried by **prepositions**. Egyptian has a limited inventory of prepositions, but each one is highly polysemous, requiring context to pin down the right translation. Here are five of the core prepositions.

## m: the most frequent preposition

**m** is Egyptian's single most frequently used preposition, covering an unusually wide semantic range: "in," "from," "as," "with," and more. Depending on context it can mark location ("in"), material or instrument ("with"), or manner ("as").

## n: "to, for" (dative)

**n** functions as a dative preposition meaning "to, for." As covered in [Genitive Constructions](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/genitive-constructions/), the genitival adjective n(y) used in the indirect genitive is itself derived from this same n.

<p class="hieroglyph">𓈖𓆑</p>

```
n=f
to=he/him
"to him, for him" (preposition n + suffix pronoun =f)
```

## r: "to, against" (also comparative)

**r** means "to, toward, against," and is also used in comparative constructions such as "A is greater r B" — functioning much like English "than."

## ḥr: "upon, because of" (originally "face")

**ḥr** means "upon, on, because of." It derives from the noun ḥr "face" (Table 2's D2 sign). The shift from a body-part word for "face" to a spatial preposition meaning "upon" is a well-attested cross-linguistic pattern, in which body-part vocabulary commonly develops into prepositions and spatial expressions.

## ẖr: "under, carrying"

**ẖr** means "under," and is also used with an associative sense of "carrying, bearing."

## Prepositions take suffix pronouns directly

When the object of one of these prepositions is a pronoun, a [suffix pronoun](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/suffix-pronouns/) attaches directly to the preposition. As already seen with n=f "to him," the pattern "preposition + suffix pronoun" is one of the most frequent basic combinations found in Egyptian sentences.
