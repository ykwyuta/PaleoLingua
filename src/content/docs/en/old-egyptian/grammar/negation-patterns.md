## Negation depends on sentence type

Old Egyptian does not have a single all-purpose negative word like English "not." Instead, it uses different negators depending on the **type of sentence** being negated. The two central negators are **n** and **nn**, supplemented by a narrower negative expression, **nfr**.

## n: negating verb forms

**n** is the most basic negator, used in the "negative verb form" construction that negates verb forms such as [sḏm=f](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/sdm-f-verb-form/). It is placed directly before the verb and states that the action did not occur.

<p class="hieroglyph">𓈖𓋴𓆓𓅓𓆑</p>

```
n sḏm=f
neg. hear=he
"he did not hear" (n negating sḏm=f)
```

n is also used in certain constructions that negate a whole clause, making it the central negator for verbal negation.

## nn: negating adverbial and nominal sentences

**nn** negates the Adverbial Sentence type ("NP is at/in Y," covered in [Adverbial and Nominal Sentences](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/adverbial-nominal-sentences/)) and some Nominal Sentences and other non-verbal predications. It also negates certain verb forms.

<p class="hieroglyph">𓈖𓈖𓊪𓂋𓆑</p>

```
nn pr=f
neg. house=his
"he has no house" (nn negating a non-verbal sentence, illustrating the pattern)
```

The basic division of labor to remember: n mainly negates verb forms of the sḏm=f type, while nn negates non-verbal predications such as Adverbial and Nominal Sentences.

## nfr: a secondary negative-existential expression

**nfr** (the same shape as the root meaning "good, beautiful, perfect") is also used as a negator, with a negative-existential or negative-completion nuance — "there is not," "not yet." It is a secondary, narrower negation strategy compared to n and nn, restricted to more limited constructions, and its precise syntactic conditions remain an area of ongoing discussion among Egyptologists. The essential point to take away here is simply that one of the roots used in negation shares its shape with nfr "good."
