## The Four Finite Forms of the G-Stem

The G-stem (basic stem) introduced in [Overview of the Verbal Stems](/en/akkadian/grammar/verbal-stems-overview/) further divides into four finite (person-inflected) forms according to tense and aspect. Using the third masculine singular of the root `PRS` "to cut, separate, decide" as an example:

| Form | Transliteration (3ms) | Cuneiform | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preterite | iprus | 𒅁𒊒𒊻 | "he decided" |
| Present/Durative | iparras | 𒄿𒉺𒅈𒊏𒀸 | "he decides" |
| Perfect | iptaras | 𒅁𒋫𒊏𒀸 | "he has (already) decided" |
| Stative/Permansive | paris | 𒉺𒊑𒄑 | "it is decided / has been settled" |

The detailed person conjugation and uses of the stative are treated separately in [The Stative/Permansive](/en/akkadian/grammar/stative-permansive/). Here we focus on the full person paradigm of the **Preterite**, which forms the narrative backbone of Akkadian verbs.

## The Preterite Person Paradigm

The preterite is formed by attaching person prefixes (and, for some persons, suffixes) around the root. Building on the preterite base `-prus-` of root `PRS`:

| Person | Transliteration | Cuneiform | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1cs | aprus | 𒀊𒊒𒊻 | a- (1cs) + prus | "I decided" |
| 2ms | taprus | 𒋫𒀊𒊒𒊻 | ta- (2ms) + prus | "you (m.) decided" |
| 2fs | taprusī | 𒋫𒀊𒊒𒋛 | ta- (2fs) + prus + -ī (fem. sg. suffix) | "you (f.) decided" |
| 3ms | iprus | 𒅁𒊒𒊻 | i- (3ms) + prus | "he decided" |
| 3fs | taprus | 𒋫𒀊𒊒𒊻 | ta- (3fs) + prus | "she decided" |
| 1cp | niprus | 𒉌𒅁𒊒𒊻 | ni- (1cp) + prus | "we decided" |
| 2cp | taprusā | 𒋫𒀊𒊒𒊓𒀀 | ta- (2cp) + prus + -ā (plural suffix) | "you (pl.) decided" |
| 3mp | iprusū | 𒅁𒊒𒋢 | i- (3mp) + prus + -ū (masc. pl. suffix) | "they (m.) decided" |

One point worth flagging: **2ms and 3fs are identical**, both surfacing as `taprus` with the prefix ta-. This is a well-known feature of the Akkadian person-marking system: in running text, whether `taprus` means "you (m.) decided" or "she decided" has to be worked out from an overt subject or from context, not from the verb form alone. Note also how the feminine singular suffix -ī and the plural suffixes -ā (2nd person) and -ū (3rd masculine) combine with the prefixes to give the fine-grained person/gender/number distinctions.

## What Distinguishes the Four Forms in Meaning and Use

- **Preterite (iprus)**: the default form for narrating discrete events in sequence, in chronicles, letters, and similar texts — a straightforward simple past, "X did Y."
- **Present/Durative (iparras)**: covers present states, habitual actions, and general truths, and also does duty for future events, since Akkadian has no dedicated future tense.
- **Perfect (iptaras)**: formed by infixing `-ta-` into the root, this form is often used to emphasize a resultant state at the moment of speaking, or a speaker's direct involvement (as in testimony or legal effect); it is also distinguished from the preterite in certain subordinate and conditional clause types.
- **Stative/Permansive (paris)**: expresses not an action itself but the resulting *state*, and — unlike the other three forms — marks person with suffixes resembling nominal case endings rather than verbal prefixes (see [The Stative/Permansive](/en/akkadian/grammar/stative-permansive/) for details).
