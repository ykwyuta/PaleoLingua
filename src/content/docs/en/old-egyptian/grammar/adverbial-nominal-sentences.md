## Sentences without a verb

Alongside verbal sentences like [sḏm=f](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/sdm-f-verb-form/), Old Egyptian has two types of **non-verbal sentences**: the **Adverbial Sentence** and the **Nominal Sentence**.

## The Adverbial Sentence: subject + adverbial predicate

The Adverbial Sentence has the structure "subject + adverbial (prepositional) predicate," meaning "NP is [in/at/with] Y." Word order is normal, subject-first.

<p class="hieroglyph">𓊪𓂋𓆑𓄤</p>

```
pr=f nfr
house=his good
"his house is good/in good condition" (illustrating the Adverbial Sentence pattern)
```

In an Adverbial Sentence, the subject noun phrase comes first, followed by an adverbial element expressing location, state, or a similar notion (a prepositional phrase or adjectival word).

## The Nominal Sentence: equating two noun phrases

The Nominal Sentence equates two noun phrases in an "A is B" pattern. Egyptian frequently uses the demonstrative **pw** ("this/that/it") as a copula-like linker in the construction "A pw."

<p class="hieroglyph">𓈖𓍿𓂋𓊪𓅱</p>

```
nṯr pw
god this
"this is a god" (Nominal Sentence, the A pw construction)
```

Note the word order in "A pw": A, the predicate noun phrase, comes first, and pw follows. pw was originally a demonstrative meaning "this/that," but within this construction it functions as a copula-like element establishing an equative relation, "X is Y."

## Combining independent pronouns with pw

The independent pronouns covered in [Dependent and Independent Pronouns](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/dependent-independent-pronouns/) can also appear as the subject (or emphasized predicate) of a Nominal Sentence. A construction like "ntf pw" combines the independent pronoun ntf "he" with pw to form an emphatic equative sentence, roughly "it is he." Both the Adverbial and Nominal Sentence types are important mechanisms for building sentences without a verb, forming a second major pillar of Egyptian syntax alongside the verbal sḏm=f construction.
