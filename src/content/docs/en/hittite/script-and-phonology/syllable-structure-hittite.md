## Basic Syllable Patterns

The Hittite syllable is fundamentally built around a vowel (V) nucleus, flanked by consonants (C) before and/or after. The theoretical template can be represented as (C)(C)V(C)(C), with up to about two consonants on either side of the vowel.

| Pattern | Example (root or partial word form) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| V | e- (part of a demonstrative pronoun stem, etc.) | Vowel alone |
| CV | pa-, ta-, ku- | The most basic open syllable |
| CVC | kur-, tar-, pár- | A closed syllable |
| CCV(C) | šta- (e.g., the initial cluster of šta-an-za "standing") | A syllable containing a consonant cluster |

## Representing Consonant Clusters in Cuneiform

Because cuneiform syllabic signs are fundamentally limited to the V, CV, VC, and CVC types, an actual word containing a more complex consonant cluster than these types allow must be spelled by stringing together multiple signs. A word beginning with the cluster št-, for instance, may be spelled š(a)-ta-, with an otherwise unnecessary vowel inserted between the signs. In such cases, whether the vowel appearing in the spelling was actually pronounced or is merely an artifact of cuneiform's constraints (a so-called "empty vowel") must be judged carefully, word by word and context by context.

```
iš-ta-an-za-ni
heart/soul (dat.-loc.)
"to/in the heart, to/in the soul"
```

In this example, the iš- portion represents part of a stem containing the consonant cluster /išt-/, spelled with a doubled syllabic sign as iš-ta-.

## Accent and Syllables

Direct evidence for the placement of stress accent in Hittite — such as accent marks — is scarce, making a reliable reconstruction difficult. However, since [plene writing](/en/hittite/script-and-phonology/plene-writing-vowel-length/) tends to appear relatively more often in stressed syllables, its distribution is used as an indirect clue for inferring accent placement. Comparison with the accent systems of other Anatolian languages and with reconstructed Proto-Indo-European accent is also used to help estimate Hittite stress.

## Word-Final Consonant Clusters

Some Hittite noun case endings and verbal person endings contain a word-final consonant cluster (for example, the third-person plural present ending -anzi). Such clusters are likewise spelled by combining syllabic signs in cuneiform, so reconstructing the precise phonological shape of a word form requires distinguishing spelling convention from actual pronunciation.
