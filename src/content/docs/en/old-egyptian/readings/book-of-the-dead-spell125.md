## Background: a New Kingdom funerary text on papyrus

The Book of the Dead — literally "The Book of Coming Forth by Day" — is a collection of funerary spells composed from the New Kingdom onward (from roughly 1550 BCE), written on papyrus and buried with the deceased. Its content descends directly from the Coffin Texts and, before them, the Pyramid Texts (see [The Coffin Texts: An Excerpt](/en/old-egyptian/readings/coffin-texts-excerpt/) and [The Pyramid Texts: An Excerpt](/en/old-egyptian/readings/pyramid-texts-excerpt/)); together the three collections trace the evolution of Egyptian beliefs about the afterlife across two thousand years.

## Spell 125: the judgment of the dead

Among the spells of the Book of the Dead, Spell 125 is especially famous: the scene often called the "Weighing of the Heart." The deceased's heart (jb, believed to be the seat of will, conscience, and memory) is placed on one pan of a scale, while the other pan holds a feather symbolizing maat (truth, right order). The judgment takes place before Osiris, ruler of the underworld, and 42 divine judges. If the heart is lighter than, or balances with, the feather, the deceased is vindicated and granted eternal life in the afterlife. If the heart is heavier — weighed down by wrongdoing in life — it is devoured by the monster Ammit.

This is also the scene in which PaleoLingua's own mascot, the goddess Maat, makes a very direct appearance. Far from an abstract concept alone, Maat is depicted here as the truth itself being weighed on the scale.

## Example: a phrase for "maat"

The phrase below is a short illustrative example reflecting the Weighing-of-the-Heart scene, not a quotation from the actual text of Spell 125 (which is long and highly specific, and is not reproduced verbatim in this article).

<p class="hieroglyph">𓄣 𓁷 𓅓𓄿𓂝𓏏</p>

```
jb       ḥr      mꜣꜥt
heart    upon    maat (truth)
```

*jb ḥr mꜣꜥt*, "the heart upon maat (i.e., upon the scale of maat)," is an illustrative phrase. At the heart of Spell 125 lies exactly this one scene: the heart set against maat.
