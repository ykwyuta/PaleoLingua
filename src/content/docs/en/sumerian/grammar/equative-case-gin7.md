## What is the equative case?

Sumerian has a dedicated case suffix, `-gin7`, for expressing similarity or comparison — "like ~," "as ~." Corresponding to English "like" or Japanese "〜のように," this suffix is called the "equative case" and is one of the roughly ten cases surveyed in [An Overview of Case Suffixes](/en/sumerian/grammar/case-suffixes-overview/).

```
𒈗𒁶
lugal-gin7
lugal-gin7
king-EQU
"like a king"
```

The index number `7` is not pronounced; ignore it and read the word simply as "gin" (see [The Basics of Transliteration](/en/sumerian/script-and-phonology/transliteration-conventions/)).

## Stock comparisons to gods and celestial bodies

Sumerian hymns and royal inscriptions make heavy use of `-gin7` comparisons that liken a king's or a temple's radiance and power to deities such as the sun-god Utu or the sky-god An.

```
𒌓𒁶
utu-gin7
utu-gin7
Utu-EQU
"like the sun-god Utu (shining)"
```

Such imagery is a formulaic device that recurs across many hymns and inscriptions to describe a king's glory or a temple's splendor.

## Attaching to a whole noun phrase

Like other case suffixes, `-gin7` attaches only once, to the end of the entire noun phrase. Even when the phrase being compared is lengthened with a genitive chain or other modifiers, `-gin7` still marks the whole phrase as a single unit of comparison.

| Suffix | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `-gin7` | "like," "as" (similarity/comparison) |

## Summary: how it differs from other cases

Unlike the dative `-ra` (marking a recipient) or the locative `-a` (marking a location), `-gin7` does not indicate a participant's role in the clause (who did what, where). Instead, it is a rhetorical device dedicated to building comparisons between two nouns — best thought of as a purpose-built tool for figurative language rather than a case in the ordinary grammatical sense.
