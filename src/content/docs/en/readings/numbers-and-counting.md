## The sexagesimal system

Sumerian numbers are not built on a base-10 system but on a "sexagesimal" (base-60) system, combined with base-10 elements in a mixed structure. Traces of this base-60 system survive indirectly in units we still use today — 60 minutes to an hour, 60 seconds to a minute, 360 degrees in a circle.

## Cuneiform numeral signs

In early cuneiform, numbers were written with dedicated signs whose shape and size varied depending on the value they represented.

| Number | Sign shape |
|---|---|
| 1 | a small vertical wedge (a round impression made by pressing the stylus tip in at an angle) |
| 10 | a Winkelhaken (a small angular notch) |
| 60 | the same shape as 1, but a larger impression |
| 600 | the same shape as 10, but a larger impression |
| 3,600 (60×60) | an even larger circular impression |

In other words, each power of 60 reused the same basic shapes as 1 and 10, distinguished mainly by size.

## The words for 1-10

Separate from the numeral signs, Sumerian also has spoken number words.

| Number | Sumerian |
|---|---|
| 1 | diš (aš) |
| 2 | min |
| 3 | eš5 |
| 4 | limmu |
| 5 | ia2 |
| 6 | aš3 |
| 7 | imin |
| 8 | ussu |
| 9 | ilimmu |
| 10 | u |

These number words underlie the constant quantity expressions found in administrative and economic texts — "N sheep," "N tablets," and so on.
