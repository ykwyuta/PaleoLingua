## What the Construct State Is

The most basic way Akkadian expresses "the B of A" (possession or belonging) is by juxtaposing two nouns in a construction known as the **construct state**. This construction is built in two steps:

1. The possessed or "whole" noun (the **head**) is put into a special, shortened form with its case ending and mimation dropped — the construct state.
2. Immediately after it comes the possessor or limiting noun, in the **genitive** case (see [Noun Case and Mimation](/en/akkadian/grammar/noun-case-and-mimation/)).

The construct state is not an independent "case" in its own right; it is a special contextual form of the noun that presupposes a following genitive noun.

## Example: bīt šarrim "The House of the King"

Take the noun `bītum` "house" as the head and `šarrum` "king" as the possessor to form "the house of the king."

<p class="cuneiform">𒁉 𒀉    𒊭 𒅈 𒊑 𒅎</p>

```
bīt      šarrim
bītum ("house") in the construct state (case ending and mimation dropped) + šarrum ("king") in the genitive
"the house of the king"
```

Here `bītum` "house" (with its nominative case ending and mimation) shifts to `bīt` — a shortened form with both the case vowel and mimation dropped — when placed in construct. The following word "king," meanwhile, stays in its full genitive form, `šarrim` (with mimation). In other words, **only the head noun that has become construct loses its case ending**; the following genitive noun keeps its ordinary case marking.

For comparison, here is the free-standing form `bītum` "house" side by side with its construct form `bīt` "house of...":

| Form | Transliteration | Cuneiform | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free-standing (nominative) | bītum | 𒁉𒌈 | "a/the house" (can stand as subject on its own) |
| Construct | bīt | 𒁉𒀉 | "the house of..." (presupposes a following genitive noun) |

## Contrast With the ša-Periphrasis

Alongside the construct state, Akkadian has another way of expressing possession or belonging: using the invariable relative-like particle `ša` "of, that of, which."

<p class="cuneiform">𒁉 𒌈    𒊭    𒊭 𒅈 𒊑 𒅎</p>

```
bītum   ša   šarrim
bītum ("house," staying nominative with mimation) + ša ("of") + šarrim ("king," genitive)
"the house of the king" (ša-periphrasis)
```

In `bītum ša šarrim`, the head noun `bītum` does not become construct — it keeps both its case ending and mimation. Instead, the invariable particle `ša` explicitly marks the possessive/limiting relationship, followed by the genitive `šarrim`.

The construct state and the `ša`-periphrasis can convey essentially the same meaning ("the house of the king"), but the construct state is the older, more compact, and more tightly bound expression, while the `ša`-construction is more analytic — favored especially when the modifying phrase grows longer, or in registers closer to later colloquial speech. `ša` is used far more broadly than just for possession, functioning also as Akkadian's general relative pronoun; its use in building relative clauses is covered separately in [Relative Clauses with ša](/en/akkadian/grammar/relative-clauses-sa/).
