## Two Conjugation Patterns

Hittite verbs are traditionally divided into two groups, the **mi-conjugation** and the **hi-conjugation**, based on the ending of the first-person singular present. The names are simply a convenient label based on whether that ending is -mi or -ḫi.

## Comparing Present-Tense Personal Endings

| Person/number | Mi-conjugation (e.g. ēš- "to be") | Hi-conjugation (e.g. dai- "to put") |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | eš-mi | dā-ḫḫi |
| 2sg | eš-ši | dā-tti |
| 3sg | ēš-zi | dā-i |
| 1pl | eš-weni | tii̯a-weni |
| 2pl | eš-teni | tii̯a-tteni |
| 3pl | aš-anzi | tii̯-anzi |

```
ē-eš-zi
be-3SG.PRES (mi-conjugation)
"he/she/it is"

da-a-i
put-3SG.PRES (hi-conjugation)
"he/she puts it"
```

As the table shows, the clearest single clue for telling the two conjugations apart is the third-person singular ending: -zi for the mi-conjugation, versus -i (usually with a long vowel) for the hi-conjugation.

## Why Two Conjugations Exist

Regarding the origin of this two-way split, comparative Indo-European linguistics widely supports the view that the hi-conjugation endings (-ḫi, -tti, -i, etc.) derive from the Proto-Indo-European **perfect** personal endings (traditionally reconstructed as *-h₂e, *-th₂e, *-e, and so on). In other words, Hittite hi-conjugation verbs are thought to descend from forms that originally carried a perfect meaning in Proto-Indo-European — "to be in a state of having done X" — which later shifted over time to a simple present meaning.

The mi-conjugation, by contrast, is the more "classic" pattern, continuing the ordinary Proto-Indo-European present-tense personal endings (*-mi, *-si, *-ti, etc.).

## Tendencies in Meaning and Lexical Distribution

Whether a given verb belongs to the hi- or mi-conjugation is fixed lexically and cannot be fully predicted from meaning alone. That said, verbs expressing a state or a change of position ("put," "stand," "sit," etc.) have been noted to belong to the hi-conjugation relatively often, which is considered consistent with the hypothesis that the hi-conjugation originates in the PIE perfect (a category expressing a resulting state).
