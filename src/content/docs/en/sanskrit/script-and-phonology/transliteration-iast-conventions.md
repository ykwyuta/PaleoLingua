## What Is IAST?

This site uses **IAST (International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration)** for transliterating Sanskrit. IAST is a scholarly transliteration scheme that maps each Devanāgarī phoneme onto a Latin letter (often with a diacritic) in as close to a one-to-one correspondence as possible. It was established following the Congress of Orientalists held in Geneva in 1894, and it remains the standard used throughout Indological and linguistic scholarship worldwide, including in Japan.

## Marking Vowel Length

The short/long vowel contrast is marked by the presence or absence of a macron (a bar diacritic, ¯) over the vowel letter.

| Short | Long | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a | ā | rāma (the name Rāma; note the contrast between a and ā) |
| i | ī | deva "god" / devī "goddess" |
| u | ū | guru "teacher" / gurū (lengthening as seen in, e.g., dual forms) |
| ṛ | ṝ | kṛta "done" (ṛ) / pitṝṇām "of the fathers" (ṝ, comparatively rare) |

## Marking Retroflexes

Retroflex consonants (see [Retroflex Consonants](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/retroflex-consonants/)) are marked by adding a combining dot below to the corresponding dental letter.

| Dental | Retroflex |
|---|---|
| t | ṭ |
| th | ṭh |
| d | ḍ |
| dh | ḍh |
| n | ṇ |
| l | ḷ (the Vedic vocalic l; note this same symbol is sometimes reused by analogy with the retroflex nasal ṇ, distinct from the syllabic vowel ḷ) |

## Marking Sibilants and Nasals

| Sound | IAST | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Palatal sibilant | ś | s with a caron (ˇ) above |
| Retroflex sibilant | ṣ | s with a dot below (same convention as the retroflexes) |
| Palatal nasal | ñ | n with a tilde (~) above |
| Velar nasal | ṅ | n with a dot above |

## Anusvāra and Visarga

- **Anusvāra** (vowel nasalization) is written as **ṃ**, an m with a dot above.
- **Visarga** (voiceless aspiration) is written as **ḥ**, a symbol resembling two dots below.

For details on how these behave, see [Anusvāra and Visarga](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/anusvara-visarga/).

## How IAST Differs from Devanāgarī Orthography

IAST and Devanāgarī are both, in principle, designed to keep a one-to-one correspondence between phonemes and letters (or letter sequences), but they differ significantly as writing systems.

| Aspect | Devanāgarī | IAST |
|---|---|---|
| Script type | Abugida (vowels inherent in consonant letters) | Alphabetic (phonemic) script; vowel letters can never be omitted |
| Word segmentation | Spaces separate words, but sandhi often fuses word boundaries phonetically | Scholarly transliteration may render the sandhied surface form as-is, or insert hyphens at morpheme boundaries, depending on context |
| Conjuncts (consonant clusters) | Has dedicated ligature glyphs where consonants visually merge (see [Consonant Clusters and Conjuncts](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/consonant-clusters-conjuncts/)) | Simply writes consonant letters in sequence (e.g., kṣ, jñ) |

## A Brief Comparison with Other Transliteration Schemes

Besides IAST, several schemes have been devised to represent Sanskrit without diacritics, primarily for computing environments.

| Scheme | Characteristics | Example ("Kṛṣṇa") |
|---|---|---|
| IAST | Latin letters with diacritics; the standard for scholarly publication | kṛṣṇa |
| Harvard-Kyoto | Substitutes case distinctions for diacritics (e.g., ṛ→R, ś→z, ṣ→S) | kRSNa |
| ITRANS | Devised for ASCII-only input in the email era, using capital letters and multi-character sequences | kRRiShNa (representations vary by implementation) |

This site prioritizes scholarly readability and uses IAST consistently throughout. Alternate diacritic-free representations are not covered here.
