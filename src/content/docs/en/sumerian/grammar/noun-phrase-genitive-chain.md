## Order of elements within a noun phrase

A Sumerian noun phrase places the head noun first, then stacks modifying elements after it in a fixed order:

```
noun → adjective → genitive phrase (possessor-ak) → demonstrative → plural marker (-ene) → case suffix
```

Not every slot is filled in every phrase, but whenever more than one modifier is present, they always appear in this order.

## Building a genitive (possessive) phrase

To say "B's A" (where B possesses or specifies A), the possessed noun A is followed by the possessor B plus the genitive suffix `-ak`.

```
e2   lugal-ak
house king-GEN
"the king's house"
```

`-ak` surfaces as such before a following vowel, but it commonly reduces to `-a` when followed by a consonant or the end of the phrase — often with the preceding consonant doubling in its place. So "the king's house," standing at the end of a phrase with no further case suffix, is frequently written `e2 lugal-la`.

## Chaining genitives (nesting)

Genitive phrases can themselves be nested, allowing multi-level possession such as "the C of the B of A."

```
ka2       e2         lugal-ak-a(k)
gate      house-GEN  king-GEN
"the gate of the king's house"
```

Here the whole phrase `e2 lugal-ak` ("the king's house") itself functions as the genitive modifier of `ka2` ("gate"). No matter how long a genitive chain grows, the underlying structure stays the same: noun + (genitive phrase).
