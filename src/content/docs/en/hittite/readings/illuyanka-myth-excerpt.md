## A Myth Recited at the Purulli Festival

The **Illuyanka myth** tells the story of the battle between the storm-god Tarḫunna and a dragon (serpent) named Illuyanka, and is a ritual narrative recited as part of the **Purulli festival**, a spring new-year celebration. It is thought to have originally been a story accompanying a Hattic (the non-Indo-European language of Anatolia's indigenous population) ritual, and survives today through its Hittite translation. Two versions of this myth, differing in content, are recorded together.

## The First Version: The Goddess Inara and the Mortal Ḫupašiya

In the first version, the storm-god is initially defeated in battle by Illuyanka. Inara, the storm-god's daughter, seeks to turn the situation around and asks a mortal man, Ḫupašiya, for help. Inara throws a lavish feast, inviting Illuyanka and his offspring to eat and drink.

Below is a simplified example sentence, for learning purposes, conveying the gist of this scene.

```
nu-za MUŠ-il-lu-ya-an-ka-aš NINDA-an a-da-an-na
and-(reflexive) serpent(det.)-Illuyanka bread-ACC eat-INF
"and the serpent Illuyanka to eat bread"

na-aš-kán ša-ra-a Ú-UL tar-na-aš
and-he-(local particle) up not able-3SG.PRET
"and he (Illuyanka), (bloated,) could not get back up (into his hole)"
```

Having overeaten to the point of being unable to move, Illuyanka is bound with rope by Ḫupašiya, and the storm-god then appears and kills him.

## A Tragic Ending

As a reward, Inara builds Ḫupašiya a house on a cliff to live in, but instructs him never to look out the window at his own family below. One day, however, Ḫupašiya breaks this prohibition and looks outside, leading — it is said — to a tragic ending (the details of this part are lost due to damage to the text).

## The "Dragon-Slaying" Pattern Common Across Indo-European Myth

The narrative pattern in which a storm-god or thunder-god battles and slays a dragon or serpent — sometimes called "chaoskampf" ("battle against chaos") in English — is found widely across Indo-European mythology, not just in the Hittite Illuyanka myth. The battle between the thunder-god Indra and the serpent Vṛtra in Vedic mythology, and between the thunder-god Thor and the great serpent Jörmungandr in Norse mythology, are frequently compared typologically to this story. Such similarities have been suggested to reflect a shared mythological motif among speakers of Proto-Indo-European, though the possibility of later resemblance through cultural contact cannot be ruled out, and a definitive conclusion should be avoided.
