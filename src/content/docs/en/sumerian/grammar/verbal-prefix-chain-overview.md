## Looking one level deeper into the "prefix chain"

[Person Affixes and the Basic Verb Structure](/en/sumerian/grammar/personal-affixes-verb-basics/) introduced the Sumerian finite verb's basic skeleton — "(prefix chain) - root - (suffix)" — and noted that the prefix portion stacks elements for mood, direction, and person. This article goes one step further and looks, in simplified form, at how that "prefix chain" is analyzed as a sequence of slots.

## A simplified slot model

The precise analysis of Sumerian's verbal prefix chain differs from scholar to scholar in its details and remains an actively debated field. What follows is one simplified, representative ordering of slots, intended to give beginners a general picture.

| Order | Slot | Main role | Representative example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ① | Modal prefix | Marks the speaker's stance — wish, negation, a future-leaning nuance, etc. | `nu-` (negation), `ha-` (precative/wish) |
| ② | Directional/voice prefix | Marks direction of the action (ventive, etc.) or voice (middle, etc.) | `mu-` (ventive), `ba-` (middle) |
| ③ | Person/case prefix | Cross-references arguments such as the dative or locative — "to whom," "where" | Person prefixes corresponding to dative/locative arguments |
| ④ | Root | The verb's core meaning | `du3` (build) |
| ⑤ | Person suffix | Person/number of the absolutive argument (subject/object) | `-en` (1st/2nd singular) |

## Fitting mu-du3 into the slots

Take `mu-du3` ("he built it"), which has appeared repeatedly in earlier articles: it breaks down into `mu-` (slot ②, directional/voice — ventive) plus `du3` (slot ④, the root) — a relatively simple combination.

```
𒈬𒁺
mu-du3
mu-du3
VENT-build
"he built it (toward here)"
```

In more complex sentences, slots ① through ③ can all be filled at once, so a single verb form can carry information about who, to whom, where, and in which direction — all stacked together.

## Why this is called "polysynthetic"

This stacking of multiple slots into a single verb form is exactly what lies behind the "polysynthetic" trait mentioned in [Person Affixes and the Basic Verb Structure](/en/sumerian/grammar/personal-affixes-verb-basics/). Information that English or Japanese would spread across several separate words (pronouns, prepositions, particles) is instead compressed into the verb's prefix slots in Sumerian.

## A note on how to proceed

The slot model above is a simplified representative example only; scholars (Falkenstein, Thomsen, Jagersma, Attinger, and others) differ on the exact number and boundaries of the slots. This site's goal is first to get comfortable with the idea that "prefixes fall into several slots occurring in a fixed order" — the details of individual slots are covered in related articles, such as [mu- versus ba-/bi2-](/en/sumerian/grammar/ventive-and-middle-prefixes/) and [The Negative Prefix nu-](/en/sumerian/grammar/negation-nu/).
