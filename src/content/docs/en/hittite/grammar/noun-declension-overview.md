## Two Genders: Common and Neuter

Hittite nouns fall into two grammatical **genders**:

- **Common gender**: a class close to the traditional notion of "animate," covering people, animals, and the like. It is thought to represent a merger of what corresponds to the masculine/feminine distinction in other Indo-European languages.
- **Neuter**: a class close to the traditional notion of "inanimate," covering things and abstract concepts.

Unlike the three-gender system (masculine/feminine/neuter) found in Latin or Greek, this two-gender system is thought to reflect an older stage of Indo-European characteristic of the Anatolian branch. The special syntactic behavior of neuter nouns is discussed in detail in [The Neuter Ergative Case](/en/hittite/grammar/neuter-ergative-case/).

## The Cases and Their Functions

| Case | Main function | Example singular ending (antuḫša- "man") |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | Subject of an intransitive verb; subject of a transitive verb (common gender) | antuḫšaš |
| Accusative | Direct object of a transitive verb | antuḫšan |
| Genitive | Possession/belonging ("of") | antuḫšaš |
| Dative-Locative | Indirect object ("to"), location ("at/in") | antuḫši |
| Ablative | Source ("from"), standard of comparison, instrument (later) | antuḫšaz |
| Instrumental | Means/instrument ("by/with") | antuḫšit |
| Ergative | The special case marking a neuter noun as the subject of a transitive verb | (attested only on neuter nouns) |

The merger of the dative and locative into a single case, and the extension of the ablative to cover not only source but also comparison and instrument, are notable features of the Hittite case system.

## Example: The Declension of antuḫša- "man" (Singular)

```
antuḫša-aš
man-NOM
"the man" (subject)

antuḫša-an
man-ACC
"the man" (object)

antuḫša-i
man-DAT.LOC
"to the man; at the man"
```

## On the Plural

The plural uses a different combination of endings than the singular. In many stem classes the nominative and accusative plural differ from each other, while the genitive plural and dative-locative plural tend to share endings distinct from those of the nominative/accusative. The details of the plural endings, and any stem alternations (such as syllable loss) that occur before the endings, depend on the stem class and must be checked lexeme by lexeme.

## Identical Nominative and Accusative in Neuter Nouns

As in Indo-European languages generally, Hittite neuter nouns show **identical nominative and accusative forms** — the same word form is used whether the neuter noun is the subject of an intransitive verb or the object of a transitive one. The special case marking (the ergative) that appears only when a neuter noun serves as the subject of a transitive verb is covered in the next article, [The Neuter Ergative Case](/en/hittite/grammar/neuter-ergative-case/).
