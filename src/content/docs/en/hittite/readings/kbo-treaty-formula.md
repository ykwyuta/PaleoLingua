## The Abbreviations KBo and KUB

Scholarly articles on Hittite treaties frequently cite texts using the abbreviations **KBo** (Keilschrifttexte aus Boghazköi, "Cuneiform Texts from Boğazköy") and **KUB** (Keilschrifturkunden aus Boghazköi, "Cuneiform Documents from Boğazköy"), followed by a number. These are the names of German scholarly publication series collecting the cuneiform tablets excavated at Ḫattuša (Boğazköy). Most Hittite treaty documents are known today through such numbered tablet publications.

## The Standard Structure of Vassal Treaties

Treaties concluded between a Hittite Great King and the rulers of surrounding vassal states — so-called "vassal treaties" — share a remarkably consistent structure.

| Component | Content |
|---|---|
| Preamble | Names the Great King issuing the treaty and his titles |
| Historical prologue | Narrates the history of relations between the two parties, justifying the treaty |
| Stipulations | Lists the vassal's specific obligations (military cooperation, extradition of fugitives, etc.) |
| Deposit/reading clause | Requires a copy of the treaty to be kept in a temple and read aloud periodically |
| Divine witness list | Lists the gods of both lands as witnesses to the treaty |
| Blessings and curses | Declares blessings for keeping the treaty and divine curses for breaking it |

## The Formula of the Divine Witness List

The list of gods at the end of a treaty often names the principal deities of each land at considerable length.

```
DINGIR.MEŠ ku-i-e-eš tup-pí-ya-za ka-tan
gods who tablet-in (local particle)
"the gods named in this document"

nu-uš-ma-aš iš-ta-ma-aš-du-uš
and-to.them listen-IMP.3PL
"may (those gods) give ear to them (the two parties)"
```

## The Formula of Blessing and Curse

Every treaty closes with a paired formula of blessing for compliance and curse for violation.

```
ku-iš ke-e up-pí-ya-uš ḫa-an-da-a-iz-zi
whoever these words-ACC.PL keep-3SG.PRES
"whoever keeps these words (stipulations)"

nu-uš-ši DINGIR.MEŠ ke-e-el li-in-ga-aš a-ša-an-du
to.him gods this.oath's be-IMP.3PL
"may the gods of this oath stand (with) him"
```

## Why Such Rigid Formulas?

The remarkably uniform structure of these treaty texts reflects the need, given the nature of a treaty as a document, to make its legitimacy and binding force unmistakably clear. By listing the gods as witnesses and spelling out blessings and curses explicitly, a treaty was given the weight of a religious oath, not merely a political agreement. This structure is sometimes noted for its similarity to the form of covenant narratives in the later Hebrew Bible, and is often cited as a good example of how widespread the "treaty" as a document genre was across the ancient Near East.
