## The Ṛgveda and Its Opening Hymn

The **Ṛgveda** ("knowledge of praise-verses") is the oldest of the four Vedas and forms their textual core, comprising 1,028 hymns (*sūktas*) arranged in ten books (*maṇḍalas*). Its dating is debated, but the core "family books" (Books 2–7) are often placed around 1500–1200 BCE, while peripheral books (1 and 10, for instance) are thought to contain somewhat later material. The final compilation of the whole is usually dated to around 1200 BCE or somewhat later.

**Hymn 1** of Book 1, which opens the entire collection in its traditional arrangement, is addressed to **Agni**, the god of the sacrificial fire (cognate with Latin *ignis*, "fire"). Agni is praised as the mediator who carries offerings to the gods in heaven — the deified embodiment of the officiating priest himself.

## The Text (Verses 1–2)

The text below follows the standard Śākala recension (traditional Vedic pitch-accent marks vary in notation across manuscripts and editions, so they are omitted here and discussed conceptually below).

```
अग्निमीळे पुरोहितं यज्ञस्य देवमृत्विजम् ।
होतारं रत्नधातमम् ॥

agnim īḷe purohitaṃ yajñasya devam ṛtvijam |
hotāraṃ ratnadhātamam ||
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Devanāgarī | IAST | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| अग्निम् | agnim | agni- ("Agni," fire-god) + -m (masc. sg. accusative) | Agni (acc.) |
| ईळे | īḷe | √īḍ "to praise," 1sg. present middle | I praise |
| पुरोहितम् | purohitam | puras "before" + hita "placed" → "domestic priest" (acc.) | the household priest (acc.) |
| यज्ञस्य | yajñasya | yajña "sacrifice," genitive sg. | of the sacrifice |
| देवम् | devam | deva "god," accusative sg. | the god (acc.) |
| ऋत्विजम् | ṛtvijam | ṛtu "season/ritual time" + yaj "to sacrifice" → "one who sacrifices in season" (acc.) | the ritual priest (acc.) |
| होतारम् | hotāram | agent noun of √hu "to pour/offer" (acc.) | the invoker-priest (hotṛ) (acc.) |
| रत्नधातमम् | ratna-dhā-tamam | ratna "treasure/boon" + dhā "to give/bestow" + -tama (superlative suffix) | the one who bestows treasure most bountifully |

**Translation (verse 1):** "I praise Agni, the household priest, the divine ministrant of the sacrifice, the invoking priest (hotṛ), the one who most bountifully bestows treasure."

```
अग्निः पूर्वेभिर्ऋषिभिरीड्यो नूतनैरुत ।
स देवाँ एह वक्षति ॥

agniḥ pūrvebhir ṛṣibhir īḍyo nūtanair uta |
sa devāṃ eha vakṣati ||
```

| Devanāgarī | IAST | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| अग्निः | agniḥ | agni, nominative sg. | Agni (nom.) |
| पूर्वेभिः | pūrvebhiḥ | pūrva "former/ancient," instrumental pl. | by the ancient (seers) |
| ऋषिभिः | ṛṣibhiḥ | ṛṣi "seer," instrumental pl. | by the seers |
| ईड्यः | īḍyaḥ | gerundive (future passive participle) of √īḍ "to praise," nom. sg. | to be praised |
| नूतनैः | nūtanaiḥ | nūtana "new/present," instrumental pl. | by the present (seers) also |
| उत | uta | conjunctive particle | also |
| सः | saḥ | demonstrative pronoun, nom. sg. | he (Agni) |
| देवान् | devān | deva "god," acc. pl. (sandhi form devāṃ) | the gods (acc.) |
| इह | iha | adverb | here |
| वक्षति | vakṣati | √vah "to carry," 3sg. future | will carry/bring |

**Translation (verse 2):** "Agni is to be praised by the ancient seers as well as by present ones. May he bring the gods here."

## Grammatical and Phonological Highlights

This hymn preserves several archaic Vedic forms. The verb **īḷe** ("I praise") is a first-person singular present middle form; the freedom in choice of voice — where Classical Sanskrit might more predictably expect an active form for a comparable meaning — is characteristic of Vedic usage. **ईड्यः īḍyaḥ** is a verbal adjective (gerundive, or "future passive participle") expressing obligation or possibility ("to be praised"). This formation remains common throughout Classical Sanskrit, but Vedic Sanskrit shows a wider array of coexisting derivational suffixes for it (-ya, -tva, -anīya, and others).

Every line of this hymn also originally carried marks for the **Vedic pitch accent** (*svara*), distinguishing *udātta* ("raised"), *anudātta* ("not raised"), and *svarita* ("moving/circumflex") tones. In Classical Sanskrit, accent is largely lost both in writing and in speech, but in Vedic Sanskrit it played a real grammatical role — for instance in distinguishing nouns from verbs, or in disambiguating compounds.

## Ritual Significance

Agni holds a special place in Vedic ritual because he was conceived as the **mediator between heaven and earth, between humans and gods**. Offerings cast into the sacrificial fire were believed to rise as smoke and reach the gods above. The placement of the Agni hymn as the very first hymn of the Ṛgveda is generally understood to mirror the structure of Vedic ritual itself: a sacrifice begins by invoking Agni first, asking him to relay the offering and summon the other gods.
