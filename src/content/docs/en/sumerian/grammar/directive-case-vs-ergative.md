## One "-e," two identities

As noted in [An Overview of Case Suffixes](/en/sumerian/grammar/case-suffixes-overview/), Sumerian has two case suffixes that happen to look identical: `-e`. One is the "ergative" seen in [Ergative and Absolutive](/en/sumerian/grammar/sumerian-ergative/); the other is the "directive" (sometimes called locative-2), a case that marks inanimate nouns. Because the two are formally identical, this is a classic stumbling block for learners.

| Function | Typically attaches to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ergative | The agent (usually animate — a person or deity) | Marks the subject of a transitive verb |
| Directive | Inanimate nouns | "toward," "in contact with" — a spatial direction or point of contact |

## Ergative -e: marking the agent

```
𒈗𒂊     𒂍     𒈬𒁺
lugal-e  e2   mu-du3
lugal-e   e2   mu-du3
king-ERG  house  he-built
```

"The king built the house." `lugal` (king) is the agent of the transitive verb `du3` (build), an animate noun, so it takes the ergative `-e`.

## Directive -e: direction toward, or contact with, an inanimate noun

```
𒂍𒂊
e2-e
e2-e
house-DIR
"toward the house," "in contact with the house"
```

The directive `-e` appears with verbs expressing a spatial relationship to an inanimate object — such as `igi...du8` ("to look at," literally "to open the eyes toward"). Unlike the ergative, it is not normally used with animate referents such as people or gods.

## Comparing with the locative -a

The same noun "house" carries a different spatial nuance depending on which case suffix it takes.

```
𒂍𒀀
e2-a
e2-a
house-LOC
"in the house" (a static location, inside)
```

The locative `-a` expresses a static "within" location, while the directive `-e` expresses "toward" or "in contact with" — a distinction covered further in [Dative, Terminative, Locative, and More](/en/sumerian/grammar/dative-terminative-locative/).

## How to tell them apart

When you see `-e`, two clues help decide which function is at play: whether the noun is animate or inanimate, and what role it plays in the clause.

| Clue | Ergative | Directive |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of the noun | Usually animate (person, deity) | Inanimate |
| Role in the clause | Agent of a transitive verb | Target of direction/contact |
