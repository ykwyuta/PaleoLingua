## Special Segments Attached to Vowels

Beyond the ordinary consonants of the [varga system](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/sanskrit-phoneme-inventory/), Sanskrit has two special sounds that occur only immediately after a vowel: **anusvāra** (अनुस्वार, "the sound that follows after") and **visarga** (विसर्ग, "emission, release"). In Devanāgarī orthography both are written as auxiliary marks attached to a vowel sign, but phonologically they are treated as independent segments.

| Name | Devanāgarī | IAST | Basic value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anusvāra | ं | ṃ | Vowel nasalization, or a nasal homorganic with the following consonant |
| Visarga | ः | ḥ | Voiceless aspiration (close to an [h] produced with roughly the mouth shape of the surrounding vowel) |

## The Phonetic Realization of Anusvāra

The actual pronunciation of anusvāra depends on what follows it.

1. **Assimilation to a following consonant's place of articulation**: before a stop, anusvāra is often realized as a nasal homorganic with that consonant. For instance, **पङ्कज** *paṅkaja* ("born from mud," i.e., a lotus) can be understood by analogy with पङ्क *paṅka* "mud," where an underlying अं + क surfaces as ṅ (a velar nasal) — in actual spelling either the nasal letter itself or the anusvāra mark ं may be used.
2. **Before a non-consonant (a vowel, or word-finally)**: in this case there is no following place of articulation to assimilate to, and anusvāra is thought to be realized as a nasalized vowel or a labial-adjacent nasal [m̃].

Anusvāra is thus notable for having no single fixed place of articulation — its realization is highly context-dependent.

## The Phonetic Realization of Visarga

Visarga ḥ is traditionally often described as a voiceless glottal fricative (similar to English h), but its precise phonetic realization has been debated. One influential view holds that visarga is realized as a **voiceless echo** of the preceding vowel. That is, the word-final ḥ in रामः *rāmaḥ* is not simply [h], but rather something closer to [aʰ] — a voiceless repetition of the preceding vowel a. This remains a live topic in Sanskrit phonetics, and its exact realization may have varied by region or recitational tradition (śākhā).

## Distribution Rules

- Anusvāra, as a rule, occurs only after a vowel and before a consonant, or word-finally. It never occurs word-initially.
- Visarga occurs mainly word-finally (and occasionally word-internally), frequently as the sandhi-derived reflex of an original word-final -s or -r. For instance, the historical masculine nominative singular ending -as regularly becomes -aḥ (visarga) in word-final position.

## Behavior in Sandhi

Both anusvāra and visarga are highly "unstable" sounds, frequently altered by the rules of [sandhi](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/sandhi-external-internal/).

- **Assimilation of anusvāra**: a word-final -m becomes anusvāra ṃ before a following consonant, and its place of articulation can then assimilate to that consonant. For example, the pausa form **रामम् गच्छति** *rāmam gacchati* is written in connected speech as **रामं गच्छति** *rāmaṃ gacchati*, where the ṃ is realized close to the place of articulation of the following consonant (here, velar, since it precedes g).
- **Sandhi of visarga**: depending on the following sound (voiced or voiceless, a vowel or not), visarga can shift to r, revert to s, or fuse with and disappear before a following vowel — a remarkably wide range of outcomes. For instance, **रामः अत्र** *rāmaḥ atra* ("Rāma [is] here") becomes, through sandhi, **रामो ऽत्र** *rāmo 'tra* (ḥ shifts to o, and the following elided a is marked with the avagraha).

In short, both anusvāra and visarga are best understood not as fixed, independent sounds, but as highly context-dependent segments that routinely change shape in the course of sandhi.
