## Two Systems of Personal Pronouns

Akkadian has two distinct systems of personal pronoun. **Independent personal pronouns** ("I," "you," "he," etc.) stand as separate words in a sentence. **Enclitic (suffix) pronouns** attach directly to the end of a noun or a verb to mark a possessor or an object. The two systems have entirely different shapes and serve different syntactic roles.

## The Independent Pronoun Paradigm

Independent pronouns are used chiefly for emphasis on the subject, or as the subject of a verbless nominal sentence ("A is B") — since finite verbs already carry person marking internally, an independent subject pronoun is not obligatory and is mainly emphatic.

| Person | Akkadian | Cuneiform | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1cs | anāku | 𒀀𒈾𒆪 | I |
| 2ms | atta | 𒀜𒋫 | you (m.) |
| 2fs | atti | 𒀜𒋾 | you (f.) |
| 3ms | šū | 𒋗𒌋 | he |
| 3fs | šī | 𒅆𒄿 | she |
| 1cp | nīnu | 𒉌𒄿𒉡 | we |
| 2mp | attunu | 𒀜𒌅𒉡 | you (m.pl.) |
| 2fp | attina | 𒀜𒋾𒈾 | you (f.pl.) |
| 3mp | šunu | 𒋗𒉡 | they (m.) |
| 3fp | šina | 𒅆𒈾 | they (f.) |

<p class="cuneiform">𒀀𒈾𒆪</p>

```
a-na-ku
I (1cs independent pronoun)
"I"
```

<p class="cuneiform">𒋗𒌋</p>

```
šu-u
he (3ms independent pronoun)
"he"
```

The table shows that 2nd and 3rd person forms distinguish masculine from feminine by their ending vowel (an `-a`-series vs. an `-i`-series), and that plural forms build on the singular stem with an added element (`-nu` for 2nd person, `-na` for feminine plural).

## Suffix Pronouns on Nouns (Possessive Suffixes)

When an enclitic pronoun attaches to a noun, it produces a possessive expression ("my," "your," etc.). Take `bītum` ("house," 𒁉𒌈 bi-tum) as an example. When a suffix pronoun attaches, the noun loses its nominative mimation (the `-m` of `-um`) and reduces to the bare stem `bīt-` before the suffix.

| Person | Suffix | Example | Cuneiform | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1cs | -ī | bītī | 𒁉𒋾 | my house |
| 2ms | -ka | bītka | 𒁉𒀉𒅗 | your (m.) house |
| 2fs | -ki | bītki | 𒁉𒀉𒆠 | your (f.) house |
| 3ms | -šu | bītšu | 𒁉𒀉𒋗 | his house |
| 3fs | -ša | bītša | 𒁉𒀉𒊭 | her house |
| 1cp | -ni | bītni | 𒁉𒀉𒉌 | our house |

<p class="cuneiform">𒁉𒋾</p>

```
bi-ti
house-my
"my house"
```

<p class="cuneiform">𒁉𒀉𒅗</p>

```
bi-it-ka
house-your(m.)
"your house"
```

<p class="cuneiform">𒁉𒀉𒊭</p>

```
bi-it-ša
house-her
"her house"
```

Swapping the suffix on `bītum` alone conveys the possessor. Note that the 3rd-person suffixes share their initial consonant (š-) with the corresponding independent pronouns `šū`/`šī` (`-šu` "his," `-ša` "her").

## The Same Suffixes on Verbs: Direct Objects

The very same set of enclitic pronouns also attaches directly to finite **verb** forms, where it marks a direct object ("him," "her," "it"). For example, adding the 3ms object suffix `-šu` ("him") to `iprus` ("he decided," the G-stem preterite) gives `iprusšu` ("he decided him / settled the matter of him").

<p class="cuneiform">𒅁𒊒𒊻𒋗</p>

```
ip-ru-us-šu
he.decided-him
"he decided him"
```

Whether the same suffix attaches to a noun or a verb changes its meaning — possessor versus object — but the shapes of the suffixes themselves are largely shared, showing that Akkadian's pronoun system operates as one coherent set across both the noun phrase and the verb phrase.
