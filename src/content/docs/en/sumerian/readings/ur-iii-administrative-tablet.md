## The real stars of the surviving corpus

As noted in [What Is Sumerian?](/en/sumerian/script-and-phonology/what-is-sumerian/), of the several hundred thousand surviving Sumerian tablets, the largest share by far consists not of literature or royal inscriptions but of administrative and economic records tracking the movement of sheep, grain, and labor. Under the Third Dynasty of Ur (roughly the 22nd-21st centuries BCE), in particular, a highly centralized state bureaucracy generated enormous numbers of documents recording the movement of livestock and goods in minute detail.

## Example: a line recording a livestock disbursement

The line below is a simplified, illustrative composite of a pattern common in Ur III livestock records — not a word-for-word quotation of any single document.

```
𒌋      𒇻    𒌨𒀭𒀏𒋫         𒁀𒍣
1(u)    udu   ur-dnanše-ta   ba-zi
1(u)    udu   ur-dnanše-ta   ba-zi
10      sheep  Ur-Nanše-ABL   it.was.withdrawn
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Word | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1(u) | numeral sign (10) | 10 (head) |
| udu | noun | sheep |
| ur-`d`nanše-ta | proper noun + -ta (ablative) | from (the account managed by) Ur-Nanše |
| ba-zi | ba- (middle voice) + zi (to withdraw, to go out) | was withdrawn |

**Japanese**: 「羊10頭、ウル・ナンシェ（の管理する在庫）から払い出された。」
**English**: "10 sheep, withdrawn from (the account of) Ur-Nanše."

## Administrative texts as a formulaic genre

Entries like this typically repeat a short, fixed pattern — "quantity + item + a named party (often with the ablative `-ta` or dative `-ra`) + verb (`ba-zi`, "was withdrawn"; `mu-DU`, "was delivered"; etc.)" — and in an actual document, this is usually followed by a date (a year name) and the name of the responsible official.

## Vocabulary and structural notes

- The use of `-ta` (ablative) to mark a starting point ("from ~") is the same usage seen in [Dative, Terminative, Locative, and More](/en/sumerian/grammar/dative-terminative-locative/).
- The `ba-` in `ba-zi` is the middle-voice prefix covered in [The Ventive Prefix mu- and the Middle-Voice Prefix ba-/bi2-](/en/sumerian/grammar/ventive-and-middle-prefixes/), carrying a nuance closer to "it went out (from stock, on its own)" than "someone actively took it out."
- As seen in [The Actual Glyphs of the Cuneiform Numeral Signs](/en/sumerian/script-and-phonology/cuneiform-numeral-signs/), administrative texts conventionally write a quantity like `1(u)` (10) with a dedicated numeral sign rather than spelling out the number word.
