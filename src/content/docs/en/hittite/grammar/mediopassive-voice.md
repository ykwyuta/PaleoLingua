## Active and Mediopassive

Independently of the [mi-conjugation/hi-conjugation](/en/hittite/grammar/mi-conjugation-hi-conjugation/) split, Hittite verbs also distinguish **voice**. There are two voices, **active** and **mediopassive**, and as the name suggests, the mediopassive combines both of the functions widely found across Indo-European under the labels "middle" (an action whose result affects the subject itself) and "passive."

## Mediopassive Personal Endings

The mediopassive has its own dedicated set of personal endings, distinct from the active.

| Person/number | Present (mediopassive) | Preterite (mediopassive) |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | -ḫa(ri) | -ḫat(i) |
| 2sg | -ta(ri) | -tat(i) |
| 3sg | -a(ri) / -ta(ri) | -at(i) / -tat(i) |
| 3pl | -anta(ri) | -antat(i) |

```
ki-ša-ri
become/happen-3SG.PRES.MP
"(it) becomes, happens"
```

## The Mediopassive Expressing Passive Meaning

The mediopassive can be used with a meaning close to the passive voice, promoting the object of the corresponding active verb to subject position.

```
Active: kuen-zi "he kills"
Mediopassive: kunna-ri "he/it is killed"
```

Here, what was the object of the active sentence appears as the subject of the mediopassive sentence, and the agent (who did the killing) is not necessarily specified — behavior typical of passive constructions.

## Verbs Attested Only in the Mediopassive (Deponents)

Some Hittite verbs have no corresponding active form and appear only in mediopassive shape — corresponding to what classical grammar calls "deponent verbs." The verb kiš- "to become, to happen," for instance, is normally used only in mediopassive forms (kišari, etc.), with essentially no corresponding active forms in use. For such verbs, the mediopassive is thought to no longer carry an active "passive" or "affecting oneself" meaning, but to have simply become fixed as that verb's basic conjugation.

## Its Place in Comparative Indo-European Linguistics

The active/mediopassive binary itself is thought to reflect an old system, inherited from Proto-Indo-European, also seen in Ancient Greek and Sanskrit. Hittite's mediopassive endings (-ḫa, -ta, -a, etc.) are thought to correspond genetically to the mediopassive endings of those languages, and together with the [mi-/hi-conjugation](/en/hittite/grammar/mi-conjugation-hi-conjugation/) contrast, they serve as further evidence that Hittite's verbal system inherited the complex morphology of Proto-Indo-European in its own distinctive way.
