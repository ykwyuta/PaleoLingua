## Negating a verb with nu-

The most basic way to negate a Sumerian verb is to place the prefix `nu-` near the front of the verbal prefix chain.

<p class="cuneiform">𒉡𒆕</p>

```
nu-du3
nu-du3
NEG-build
"he did not build (it)"
```

Compare this with `mu-du3` ("he built it"): swapping just `mu-` (ventive) for `nu-` (negative) flips the sentence's meaning entirely.

## Where nu- sits in the prefix chain

In the slot model from [The Verbal Prefix Chain: A Structural Overview](/en/sumerian/grammar/verbal-prefix-chain-overview/), `nu-` belongs to slot ① (the modal prefix), which sits before slot ② (directional/voice prefixes such as `mu-` or `ba-`). Because of this, `nu-` doesn't only attach directly to the root — it can also co-occur with `mu-` or `ba-`, standing at the front of a longer prefix sequence like `nu-mu-...`.

| Slot order | ① Mood | ② Direction/voice | ③ Person/case | ④ Root |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | `nu-` | `mu-` | (depends on arguments) | `du3` |

## Negation as a kind of mood

The fact that `nu-` occupies slot ① shows that negation in Sumerian isn't treated as a purely logical operation ("not ~"), but is grammatically classified alongside the "speaker's stance" mood prefixes discussed in [The Verbal Prefix Chain: A Structural Overview](/en/sumerian/grammar/verbal-prefix-chain-overview/). Other modal prefixes, such as `ha-` (precative/wish), occupy that same slot ①.
