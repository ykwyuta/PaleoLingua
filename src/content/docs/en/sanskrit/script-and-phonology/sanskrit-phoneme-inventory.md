## Vowels

Sanskrit vowels rest on a basic short/long opposition, plus a set of diphthongs (historically classed among the long vowels).

| Type | Short | Long |
|---|---|---|
| Monophthongs | a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ | ā, ī, ū, ṝ (, ḹ) |
| Diphthongs | — | e, ai, o, au |

- **a / ā**: the highest-frequency vowel. Short a is thought to have been pronounced close to a mid-central schwa-like vowel [ə].
- **i / ī, u / ū**: short/long pairs of a front close vowel and a back close vowel, respectively.
- **ṛ / ṝ**: syllabic r ([r̥]), functioning as the syllable nucleus — found, for instance, in the root √dṛś "to see."
- **ḷ**: syllabic l. Somewhat more common in Vedic; in Classical Sanskrit it survives in only a handful of forms, such as the root √kḷp "to arrange."
- **e, o**: long vowels historically derived from the diphthongs *ai, *au (synchronically they behave as monophthongs in Sanskrit).
- **ai, au**: genuine diphthongs.

Vowel length is a phonemic (meaning-distinguishing) contrast, and knowing it is also essential for understanding Sanskrit meter (*chandas*).

## Consonants — The Varga System

Sanskrit stops and nasals form a highly systematic grid called the **varga** (वर्ग *varga*, "class") system, with five sounds arranged neatly at each of five places of articulation. This classification is a product of the sophisticated phonetic analysis achieved by ancient Indian grammarians, most famously [Pāṇini](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/what-is-sanskrit/).

| Place of articulation | Voiceless unaspirated | Voiceless aspirated | Voiced unaspirated | Voiced aspirated | Nasal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Velar (kaṇṭhya) | k | kh | g | gh | ṅ |
| Palatal (tālavya) | c | ch | j | jh | ñ |
| [Retroflex (mūrdhanya)](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/retroflex-consonants/) | ṭ | ṭh | ḍ | ḍh | ṇ |
| Dental (dantya) | t | th | d | dh | n |
| Labial (oṣṭhya) | p | ph | b | bh | m |

Each row corresponds to a place of articulation, and each column to a manner (voiceless unaspirated, voiceless aspirated, voiced unaspirated, voiced aspirated, nasal). This 5×5 matrix constitutes the so-called **five vargas** (velar, palatal, retroflex, dental, and labial). The voiced aspirates (gh, jh, ḍh, dh, bh) are unfamiliar to English speakers, but in Sanskrit they function as fully independent phonemes symmetrical with the voiceless aspirates.

## Semivowels (Antaḥstha)

| Devanāgarī | IAST | Description |
|---|---|---|
| य | y | palatal approximant |
| र | r | alveolar trill (or tap) |
| ल | l | alveolar lateral approximant |
| व | v | labio-velar approximant (intermediate between English w and v) |

Each semivowel is articulated close to a corresponding vowel (y⇔i, r⇔ṛ, l⇔ḷ, v⇔u), and alternations between a vowel and its corresponding semivowel are frequent in the rules of [vowel gradation (guṇa and vṛddhi)](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/vowel-gradation-guna-vrddhi/) and [sandhi](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/sandhi-external-internal/).

## Sibilants and h

| Devanāgarī | IAST | Place of articulation |
|---|---|---|
| श | ś | palatal sibilant |
| ष | ṣ | retroflex sibilant |
| स | s | dental sibilant |
| ह | h | glottal fricative (generally described as voiced) |

The three-way distinction among sibilants by place of articulation (palatal, retroflex, dental) is one of the striking features of the Sanskrit phonological system.

## Anusvāra and Visarga

Sanskrit also has two special segments that occur only after a vowel and lack an independent place of articulation of their own: the nasalizing **anusvāra** (ṃ) and the voiceless aspirate **visarga** (ḥ). Both are positionally restricted phonemes that never occur word-initially. See [Anusvāra and Visarga](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/anusvara-visarga/) for details.

## Full Phoneme Inventory Summary

| Category | Phonemes |
|---|---|
| Vowels (short) | a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ |
| Vowels (long) | ā, ī, ū, ṝ, e, ai, o, au |
| Velars | k, kh, g, gh, ṅ |
| Palatals | c, ch, j, jh, ñ |
| Retroflexes | ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ |
| Dentals | t, th, d, dh, n |
| Labials | p, ph, b, bh, m |
| Semivowels | y, r, l, v |
| Sibilants | ś, ṣ, s |
| Other | h, ṃ (anusvāra), ḥ (visarga) |
