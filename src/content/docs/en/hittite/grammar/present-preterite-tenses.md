## Just Two Tenses

Finite Hittite verbs (those inflected for person and number) distinguish only two tenses: the **present** and the **preterite** (simple past). There is no independent future or perfect tense marked through verb morphology, unlike English or Latin.

- **Present**: covers not only current states and actions but also habitual events, and, depending on context, planned or intended future events.
- **Preterite**: a single past tense covering events and states that occurred in the past. The verb's morphology itself makes no grammatical distinction comparable to English's simple past versus perfect.

When a future event or a completed state needs to be made explicit, this is supplied by temporal adverbs ("tomorrow," "already," etc.), context, or particular constructions.

## Preterite Endings in the Mi- and Hi-Conjugations

The preterite takes different personal endings depending on whether a verb belongs to the [mi-conjugation or the hi-conjugation](/en/hittite/grammar/mi-conjugation-hi-conjugation/).

| Person/number | Mi-conjugation preterite (e.g. ēš- "to be") | Hi-conjugation preterite (e.g. dai- "to put") |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | eš-un | dā-ḫḫun |
| 2sg | eš-ta | dā-šta |
| 3sg | ēš-ta | dā-š |
| 1pl | eš-uen | tii̯a-uen |
| 2pl | eš-ten | tii̯a-ten |
| 3pl | ēš-er | tii̯-er |

```
ē-eš-ta
be-3SG.PRET
"he/she/it was"

da-a-š
put-3SG.PRET
"he/she put it"
```

## Stem Alternations in the Preterite

Some verbs show a stem vowel in the preterite that differs from the present-tense stem (as with dai- → dā- in the table above). These stem-alternation patterns vary from verb to verb and must be learned individually.

## Interaction With the Neuter Ergative

Regardless of whether a verb is in the present or preterite, when the verb is transitive and its subject is a neuter noun, the ergative marking described in [The Neuter Ergative Case](/en/hittite/grammar/neuter-ergative-case/) can apply. Tense and case marking are independent grammatical categories that combine together to build a sentence.
