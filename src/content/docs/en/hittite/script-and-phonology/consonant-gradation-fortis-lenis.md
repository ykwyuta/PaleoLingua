## Single Versus Geminate Spelling

In Hittite cuneiform texts, the same consonant is sometimes written with a single sign (single spelling) and sometimes with a doubled sign (geminate spelling), and this alternation follows fairly consistent patterns depending on the word and word form. For the sound /t/, for instance, a single -ta- can be observed to contrast with a geminate -at-ta-.

## Sturtevant's Law

The American linguist Edgar Sturtevant proposed that this single/geminate contrast does not simply reflect a difference in consonant length (gemination), but instead reflects a contrast between a **"strong" (fortis)** consonant and a **"weak" (lenis)** consonant. This is known as **Sturtevant's Law**.

Under this view, geminate spellings (tt, pp, kk, etc.) represent voiceless, strongly articulated "fortis" consonants, while single spellings (t, p, k, etc.) represent voiced, weakly articulated "lenis" consonants. In other words, the traditional voiceless/voiced contrast is thought to be reflected in Hittite spelling as a geminate-versus-single contrast.

```
e-ša-at-ta   (fortis: tt)
sit (mediopassive) - 3SG.PRES
"he/she is sitting"

e-ša-ri   (an example from a lenis-favoring environment)
sit (mediopassive) - 3SG.PRES (different phonological environment)
```

(These examples are simplified for illustrating consonant gradation; the actual choice of word form involves several factors, including the verb's conjugation pattern.)

## Connection to the Proto-Indo-European Consonant System

This consonant gradation is generally thought to result from Hittite (and the Anatolian branch as a whole) reorganizing, in its own way, the Proto-Indo-European three-way contrast among voiceless, voiced, and voiced aspirated stops. The prevailing view is that the voiceless/voiced contrast in medial position was reflected in Hittite spelling in this new fortis/lenis guise. This phenomenon is one of the important clues for reconstructing consonant correspondences across the Indo-European languages.

## The Challenge of Inconsistent Spelling

As with [plene writing](/en/hittite/script-and-phonology/plene-writing-vowel-length/), single-versus-geminate spelling is not always fully consistent. Variation between manuscripts of the same word has been reported, so establishing the fortis/lenis contrast with certainty for any individual word form requires weighing multiple pieces of evidence together.
