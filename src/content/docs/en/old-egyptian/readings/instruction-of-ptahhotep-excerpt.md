## Background: one of the oldest wisdom texts

*The Instruction of Ptahhotep* belongs to the genre of instruction or wisdom literature known as "Sebayt," framed as the advice of the aged vizier Ptahhotep to his own son. Its content centers on conduct aligned with maat (right order, truth), on humility, and on the virtue of listening well. The best-known copy is the Middle Kingdom Papyrus Prisse (now held in the Bibliothèque nationale de France), and the text is counted among the oldest and most celebrated works of Egyptian wisdom literature.

## Central theme: the virtue of listening

The virtue *The Instruction of Ptahhotep* returns to again and again is "listening / obeying," expressed by the Egyptian verb *sḏm* ("to hear, to listen"). This concept goes beyond simply perceiving sound: it denotes the ethical stance of humbly heeding — and then following — the teaching of one's elders and the wise. Notably, this same root *sḏm* also serves as the paradigm verb used to illustrate the sḏm=f form (the basic active verb form) covered in the Grammar category — a nice case where grammar and reading come together around a single root.

## Example: a phrase for "listening" and "maat"

The phrase below is a short illustrative example reflecting the central theme of listening and maat, not a verbatim quotation from the manuscript.

<p class="hieroglyph">𓋴𓆓𓅓 𓅓𓄿𓂝𓏏</p>

```
sḏm      mꜣꜥt
listen   maat (truth, right order)
```

*sḏm mꜣꜥt*, "to listen to maat," is an illustrative phrase. If the entire message of *The Instruction of Ptahhotep* could be compressed into a single line, it might well be exactly this: listen humbly to maat.
