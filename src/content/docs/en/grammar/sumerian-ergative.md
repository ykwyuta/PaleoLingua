## What is an ergative language?

Sumerian is classified as an "ergative language." Unlike nominative-accusative languages such as English or Japanese, it marks verbal arguments differently depending on whether a noun is the agent of a transitive verb, versus the subject of an intransitive verb or the object of a transitive verb.

## Ergative (-e) and Absolutive (unmarked)

| Case | Marker | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Absolutive | unmarked (∅) | subject of intransitive verbs, object of transitive verbs |
| Ergative | `-e` | subject (agent) of transitive verbs |

### Example

```
lugal-e  e2  mu-du3
king-ERG house he-built
"The king built a house."
```

`lugal` ("king") is the agent of the transitive verb `du3` ("build"), so it takes the ergative suffix `-e`. `e2` ("house"), the object, remains unmarked in the absolutive.
