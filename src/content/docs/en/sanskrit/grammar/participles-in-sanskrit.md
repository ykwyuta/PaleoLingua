Sanskrit makes extensive use of participles (कृदन्त kṛdanta — adjectival or adverbial derivatives formed by adding suffixes to a root), and it is common for entire clauses to be built around participles without any finite (personally inflected) verb at all. This article covers three participle types: the present participle (active and middle), the past passive participle, and the absolutive (gerund).

## The Present Active Participle (-ant/-at)

Formed by adding the suffix **-ant** (weak stem -at) to the parasmaipada (active) present stem. It declines according to the consonant-stem (-ant stem) pattern.

```
भवत्- bhavat- (from bhū- "to become," present stem bhava- + -ant)
Nominative singular (masculine): भवन् bhavan
"(one who is) becoming"
```

```
गच्छन् बालकः पतति।
gacchan bālakaḥ patati.
go-PTCP.NOM boy-NOM fall-3SG.PRES
"The boy, (while) walking, falls."
```

The present active participle modifies a noun adjectivally while sharing that noun's subject role in the main clause (here, patati "he falls"), expressing accompanying circumstance ("while doing X").

## The Present Middle Participle (-māna/-āna)

The ātmanepada (middle voice) present stem takes the suffix **-māna** (for thematic verbs) or **-āna** (for athematic verbs). The middle voice carries a nuance in which the action's result reverts to the subject (reflexive or self-benefiting).

```
लभमानः labhamānaḥ (from labh- "to obtain," middle present stem labha- + -māna, nominative singular masculine)
"(one who is) obtaining"
```

```
फलं लभमानः सः हसति।
phalaṃ labhamānaḥ saḥ hasati.
fruit-ACC obtain-PTCP.MID.NOM he-NOM laugh-3SG.PRES
"Obtaining fruit, he laughs."
```

## The Past Passive Participle (-ta/-na)

The past passive participle (often called the "kta" participle) is formed by adding the suffix **-ta** or **-na** to the root — often in its weak grade — and is one of the most frequently used participles in Sanskrit. It carries both a passive sense ("having been done") and a perfective one ("having finished doing").

| Root | Past passive participle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| कृ kṛ- "to do" | कृत kṛta- | done, made |
| गम् gam- "to go" | गत gata- | gone |
| भू bhū- "to become" | भूत bhūta- | become, been |
| दृश् dṛś- "to see" | दृष्ट dṛṣṭa- | seen |
| भिद् bhid- "to split" | भिन्न bhinna- | split (the -na suffix) |

The past passive participle functions either as an ordinary adjective or as a verbal predicate in its own right. Constructions that convey a past event entirely through such a participle, without any finite verb, are extremely common in Sanskrit — especially in Classical prose.

```
रामेण फलं गृहीतम्।
rāmeṇa phalaṃ gṛhītam.
Rāma-INS fruit-NOM(N) take-PTCP.PST.PASS-NOM(N)
"The fruit was taken by Rāma."
```

This sentence contains no finite verb at all: the instrumental marking the agent (rāmeṇa) together with the past passive participle (gṛhītam, agreeing in gender/number/case — neuter nominative — with the object phalam) is enough to express a complete passive past event.

## The Absolutive (Gerund, -tvā)

The absolutive (also called the gerund, or the indeclinable participle, अव्ययं कृदन्त avyayaṃ kṛdanta) is an unchanging participial form expressing sequential action — "having done X" or "after doing X" — formed by adding the suffix **-tvā** to the root. When the root carries a preverb, **-ya** is used in place of -tvā.

```
गत्वा gatvā (gam- + tvā)
"having gone"
```

```
सः गृहं गत्वा भोजनं करोति।
saḥ gṛhaṃ gatvā bhojanaṃ karoti.
he-NOM house-ACC go-ABS food-ACC do-3SG.PRES
"Having gone home, he eats."
```

```
आगम्य āgamya (ā- + gam- + ya, using -ya instead of -tvā because the root carries a preverb)
"having come"
```

The absolutive expresses a sequence of actions sharing the same subject as the main clause, complementing the [locative absolute](/en/sanskrit/grammar/case-system-functions/) construction, which instead builds an adverbial clause with a subject distinct from the main clause.
