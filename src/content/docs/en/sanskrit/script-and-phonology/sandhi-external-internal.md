## What Is Sandhi?

**Sandhi** (सन्धि, "joining, junction") is the general term for the regular phonological changes that occur when adjacent sounds — most often vowel meets vowel, or consonant meets consonant or vowel — come into contact. It is often translated into English as "euphonic combination." In the Sanskrit grammatical tradition, sandhi is not treated merely as a natural sound-change phenomenon; Pāṇini's grammar codifies it into a precise rule system, and correctly applying sandhi is considered an essential skill for writing or speaking "correct" Sanskrit.

Sandhi is broadly divided into two types based on where it applies.

| Type | Domain of application | Sanskrit term |
|---|---|---|
| External sandhi | Word boundaries (when words are spoken in sequence within a sentence) | बाह्यसन्धि *bāhya-sandhi* |
| Internal sandhi | Boundaries between a stem and an affix (ending or suffix) | आन्तरसन्धि *āntara-sandhi* |

## External Sandhi — Sandhi at Word Boundaries

External sandhi occurs between words spoken or recited in sequence within a sentence. A word's isolated citation form (its "pausa" form, as found in dictionaries) often differs from its actual pronunciation in running speech — one of the trickiest aspects for learners.

### Examples of Vowel Sandhi

| Original form (word 1 + word 2) | After sandhi | Nature of the change |
|---|---|---|
| na + asti | nāsti | a + a → ā (fusion of like vowels) |
| tathā + iti | tathaiti | ā + i → ai (fusion of unlike vowels) |
| su + ukta | sūkta | u + u → ū |
| te + eva | ta eva | e + e → a e (a special case where a surfaces after e; in practice several interacting rules such as e→ay are involved) |

### Examples of Consonant Sandhi

| Original form | After sandhi | Nature of the change |
|---|---|---|
| tat + ca | tac ca | word-final t assimilates to the following palatal c |
| sat + jana | sajjana | word-final t assimilates to a following voiced sound, becoming j |
| rāmaḥ + gacchati | rāmo gacchati | word-final ḥ shifts to o before a voiced sound |

External sandhi is closely tied to the visarga alternations discussed in [Anusvāra and Visarga](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/anusvara-visarga/). Depending on the nature of the following sound (voiced or voiceless, vowel quality, etc.), ḥ can shift to o, r, s, and other forms.

## Internal Sandhi — Sandhi Within a Stem

Internal sandhi occurs within a single word, at the boundary between a root or stem and an affix (ending or suffix). Its rules are more complex than those of external sandhi, and lexically specific exceptions are common.

### Example 1: Retroflexion (the ṇatva/ṣatva rules)

When a stem contains r, ṛ, or ṣ, a following dental n or s can shift to the [retroflex](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/retroflex-consonants/) ṇ or ṣ.

| Original form | After sandhi | Nature of the change |
|---|---|---|
| rāma + nā (instrumental ending, ~-inā) | rāmeṇa | the stem's r triggers the ending's n to shift to ṇ (the ṇatva rule) |

### Example 2: The Interplay of Internal Sandhi and Vowel Gradation

The application of [vowel gradation (guṇa and vṛddhi)](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/vowel-gradation-guna-vrddhi/) is sometimes treated, in a broad sense, as a form of internal sandhi. When an affix attaches to a root, the strengthening of the root vowel to guṇa or vṛddhi grade is a regular alternation occurring precisely at the root–affix boundary, sharing important properties with sandhi proper.

| Root | Derived form | Nature of the change |
|---|---|---|
| √nī ("to lead") | nayati ("he/she leads") | root vowel i strengthens to guṇa e, which becomes ay before a following vowel |
| √bhū ("to be") | bhavati ("he/she is") | root vowel u strengthens to guṇa o, which becomes av before a following vowel |

## Differences Between External and Internal Sandhi

Both share the common trait of being regular changes triggered by sound contact, but they differ in the following ways.

| Aspect | External sandhi | Internal sandhi |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | Between independent words | Between a root/stem and an affix |
| Regularity | Governed by a relatively small set of general rules, applied nearly mechanically | Riddled with lexical and conjugational exceptions; requires substantial memorization |
| Reflection in writing | Devanāgarī orthography, as a rule, always reflects it (writing the post-sandhi form) | Always appears fixed as part of the word form |

A systematic understanding of sandhi rules is an essential foundation for reading real texts in the [Readings & Examples](/en/sanskrit/readings/rigveda-opening-hymn/) category.
