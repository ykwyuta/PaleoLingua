## Three pronoun systems

Beyond the suffix pronouns covered in [Suffix Pronouns](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/suffix-pronouns/), Old Egyptian has two further pronoun sets: **dependent pronouns**, used mainly as the direct object of a verb, and **independent pronouns**, used for emphasis and stand-alone reference. Together, these three systems divide up distinct grammatical roles.

## Dependent pronouns: direct objects of verbs

Dependent pronouns are used mainly as the direct object of a verb, or after certain particles.

| Person | Dependent pronoun |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | wj |
| 2nd sg. masc. | ṯw |
| 2nd sg. fem. | ṯn / ṯm (less common form) |
| 3rd sg. masc. | sw |
| 3rd sg. fem. | sy / st |
| 1st pl. | n |
| 2nd pl. | ṯn |
| 3rd pl. | sn |

## Independent pronouns: emphasis, standalone use, nominal-sentence subjects

Independent pronouns stand alone as free words and are used for emphasis ("I am the one who..."), topicalization, or as the subject of an "A pw" nominal sentence (see [Adverbial and Nominal Sentences](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/adverbial-nominal-sentences/)).

| Person | Independent pronoun |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | jnk ("I / it is I") |
| 2nd sg. masc. | ntk |
| 2nd sg. fem. | nt.t |
| 3rd sg. masc. | ntf |
| 3rd sg. fem. | nts |
| 1st pl. | jnn |
| 2nd pl. | nttn |
| 3rd pl. | ntsn |

## Comparing all three systems

Alongside the suffix pronoun paradigm from [Suffix Pronouns](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/suffix-pronouns/), here is a side-by-side comparison for 1st singular, 2nd singular masculine, and 3rd singular masculine.

| Person | Suffix pronoun | Dependent pronoun | Independent pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | =j | wj | jnk |
| 2nd sg. masc. | =k | ṯw | ntk |
| 3rd sg. masc. | =f | sw | ntf |

This three-way distinction for expressing "I," "you," and "he" is one of the most striking features of the Egyptian pronoun system. Each series has a clearly defined role: suffix pronouns mark possession and verbal subjects, dependent pronouns mark mainly verbal objects, and independent pronouns handle emphasis, stand-alone use, and nominal-sentence subjects.
