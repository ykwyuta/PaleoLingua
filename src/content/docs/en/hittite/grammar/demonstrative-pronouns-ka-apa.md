## Two Basic Demonstrative Pronouns

Hittite has several demonstrative pronouns distinguishing distance from the speaker (near versus far deixis), but two are central:

- **kā-**: "this." A near-deictic demonstrative referring to something close to the speaker or just mentioned in the immediately preceding context.
- **apā-**: "that, the aforementioned." A far-deictic and anaphoric demonstrative referring to something distant from the speaker, or pointing back to something already mentioned.

Like nouns, both inflect for gender (common/neuter) and case.

## A Concrete Example

```
kā-aš an-tu-uḫ-ša-aš
this-NOM man-NOM
"this man"

a-pa-a-aš LUGAL-uš
that-NOM king-NOM
"that king" (referring to a previously mentioned king)
```

## Apā-'s Other Job: Standing In for the Third-Person Pronoun

One striking feature of Hittite is that it has **no independent third-person personal pronoun**, unlike English "he/she/it" or Latin "is/ea/id." Instead, the far-deictic/anaphoric demonstrative apā- also does double duty as the third-person pronoun meaning "he, she, it," depending on context.

```
a-pa-a-aš pa-it
that/he-NOM go-3SG.PRET
"he (that person) went"
```

As this example shows, apā- functions on a continuum between its original demonstrative meaning ("that [already-mentioned] person/thing") and a third-person-pronoun-like use ("he/she/it"), depending on context. This double role is also of interest as an example of a broader tendency in language change: demonstratives grammaticalizing into personal pronouns.

## The Difference From Enclitic Pronouns

The enclitic pronouns discussed in [The Enclitic Pronoun Chain](/en/hittite/grammar/enclitic-pronoun-chain/) — such as -mu "to me" and -at "it" — cannot stand independently at the head of a clause. kā- and apā-, by contrast, are independent words that can be placed at any position in the sentence, and are used for emphasis or when an enclitic alone would leave the reference ambiguous.
