## Another Sumerian: Emesal

The Sumerian covered elsewhere on this site is based on the standard dialect/register known as "Emegir" (eme-gir15, literally "the tongue of princes"). Alongside it, a special register called "Emesal" (eme-sal, literally "fine/delicate tongue") is used only in certain genres and contexts — usually described not as a regional dialect but as a sociolinguistic register.

## Where Emesal appears

Emesal appears in essentially two contexts:

| Context | Description |
|---|---|
| Goddesses' speech in myths and literary works | Passages where a goddess speaks within a narrative are sometimes rendered in Emesal |
| Laments (balaĝ, eršemma, etc.) | In the lament genre, recited by priests called *gala*, the entire text — narrative portions included — is composed in Emesal |

This distribution suggests that Emesal functioned not as a regional dialect but as a stylistic register tied to specific genres and speakers.

## A vocabulary difference: nin and gašan

The relationship between Emesal and Emegir forms isn't uniform — some words show regular sound correspondences, while others are replaced by an entirely different word. A well-known example is the word for "lady, goddess."

```
𒎏
nin
ニン
(Emegir) lady, goddess
```

```
𒃽
gašan
ガシャン
(Emesal) lady, goddess
```

`nin` and `gašan` share the same meaning ("lady, goddess"), but have entirely different forms and are written with entirely different signs. This illustrates that Emesal sometimes uses its own distinct vocabulary set rather than showing a regular sound shift from the Emegir form.

## Where this fits in your studies

Emesal rarely appears in the introductory example sentences elsewhere on this site, but it is essential knowledge for reading primary sources in genres like hymns and laments. The detailed correspondence rules between Emesal and Emegir (a full inventory of regular sound shifts, for instance) remain an active area of research; for now, the key takeaway is simply that Sumerian had a special register used differently depending on speaker and genre.
