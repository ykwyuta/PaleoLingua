## The Oldest Surviving Hittite Text

The **Anitta Text** narrates the deeds of Pithana, king of Kuššara (a city in central Anatolia), and his son Anitta, and is generally regarded as preserving the oldest content among surviving Hittite texts. The original composition is thought to date to the Old Hittite period, though most surviving manuscripts are later copies from the New Hittite period. The text records, in a style close to first-person narration, how Anitta conquered neighboring city-states including Neša and Ḫattuša.

## The Conquest of Ḫattuša and Its Curse

One of the most famous passages in the Anitta Text is the **curse** Anitta is said to have left after conquering and destroying the city of Ḫattuša. Anitta reportedly declared that the wrath of the storm-god would fall upon anyone who resettled the city.

Below is a simplified example sentence, for learning purposes, based on the formulaic wording conveying the gist of this curse (note that this is not a verbatim quotation of one specific line, but a summary rendering of the text's content).

```
ku-iš-wa a-pé-e-el EGIR-an LUGAL-uš ki-ša-ri
who-QUOT he-GEN after king-NOM become-3SG.PRES.MP
"Whoever after him becomes king"

nu-wa URU-an EGIR-pa a-pí-ya i-ya-zi
and-QUOT city-ACC again there make-3SG.PRES
"and builds that city there again"

nu-wa-an-kán dU-aš tar-ḫu-i-li-uš wa-al-aḫ-du
and-QUOT-him-(local particle) storm.god-NOM mighty-NOM strike-IMP.3SG
"may the mighty Storm-god strike him"
```

## Vocabulary and Grammar Points

- **ki-ša-ri** "becomes" is the third-person singular present [mediopassive](/en/hittite/grammar/mediopassive-voice/) form of kiš- "to become, happen," a verb frequently cited as a [deponent](/en/hittite/grammar/mediopassive-voice/).
- **-wa** (the quotative particle) is the enclitic discussed in [The Enclitic Pronoun Chain](/en/hittite/grammar/enclitic-pronoun-chain/) that marks direct speech, showing that this whole passage is quoted as Anitta's own words.
- The determinative D visible in **dU-aš** ("the storm-god") is, as explained in [Determinatives in Hittite](/en/hittite/script-and-phonology/determinatives-in-hittite/), an unpronounced sign placed before a divine name.

## The Significance of This Curse Text

A "curse upon whoever rebuilds this city" of this kind is a rhetorical pattern found broadly across ancient Near Eastern inscriptional literature, one common device by which conquerors memorialized their victory as permanent. Read alongside the historical fact that the Hittite Kingdom later chose precisely this same city, Ḫattuša, as its new capital, this passage of the Anitta Text is also known for the striking irony it presents.
