## The Akṣara (Syllable) Principle

Devanāgarī (देवनागरी *devanāgarī*) belongs to the Brāhmī family of scripts and functions as an [abugida](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/consonant-clusters-conjuncts/) — a syllabic script built around the consonant sign, with vowels indicated by attached marks, rather than an alphabet in which consonants and vowels appear as equal, separate letters. The basic graphic unit is the **akṣara** ("syllable-sign"), which typically corresponds to a (consonant +) vowel syllable, and every bare consonant letter already carries a built-in vowel /a/.

## The Inherent Vowel /a/

A Devanāgarī consonant letter on its own represents a "consonant + short a" syllable, not the bare consonant alone. For example, क by itself represents *ka* (consonant k plus the inherent vowel a), not "k" in isolation. This is called the **inherent vowel**. Thus:

| Devanāgarī | IAST | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| क | ka | consonant k + inherent vowel a |
| न | na | consonant n + inherent vowel a |
| म | ma | consonant m + inherent vowel a |

A bare consonant letter is always read with a following a. To indicate a different vowel, or a consonant with no following vowel at all, a dedicated sign is required.

## Independent Vowel Letters and Dependent Vowel Signs (Mātrā)

Vowels are written in two distinct ways:

1. **Independent vowel letters**: used when a vowel appears on its own, without a preceding consonant — for instance, at the start of a word.
2. **Dependent vowel signs (mātrā)**: attached to a consonant letter to replace its inherent vowel a with a different vowel.

| Vowel | Independent letter | Mātrā (attached to k) | IAST |
|---|---|---|---|
| a (inherent) | अ | क (unmarked) | ka |
| ā | आ | का | kā |
| i | इ | कि | ki |
| ī | ई | की | kī |
| u | उ | कु | ku |
| ū | ऊ | कू | kū |
| ṛ | ऋ | कृ | kṛ |
| e | ए | के | ke |
| ai | ऐ | कै | kai |
| o | ओ | को | ko |
| au | औ | कौ | kau |

A mātrā may be placed to the right, left, above, or below the consonant letter (for instance, the mātrā for i is written to the **left** of the consonant even though it is pronounced after it — a mismatch between visual order and pronunciation order that learners should note). The full vowel system is discussed in more detail in [The Sanskrit Phoneme Inventory](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/sanskrit-phoneme-inventory/).

## The Virama (Vowel-Suppression Mark)

To represent a "bare" consonant with no following vowel — for example, a word-final consonant, or a consonant that will form part of a cluster — a small diagonal stroke called the **virama** (विराम *virāma*, also called *halant*) ् is written beneath the consonant letter.

| Form | Reading | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| क् | k (no vowel) | the virama suppresses the inherent vowel a |
| क | ka | no virama; the inherent vowel a is retained |

The virama is also the mechanism underlying **conjunct consonants (ligatures)**, formed when two or more consonants combine into a single compound glyph. This is discussed in detail in [Consonant Clusters and Conjuncts](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/consonant-clusters-conjuncts/).

## Basic Consonant Inventory

Sanskrit consonant letters are arranged in the traditional "varga" system, organized systematically by place of articulation. The full phonological classification is covered in [The Sanskrit Phoneme Inventory](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/sanskrit-phoneme-inventory/); here we present only the letter-to-sound correspondences.

| Devanāgarī | IAST | Devanāgarī | IAST | Devanāgarī | IAST | Devanāgarī | IAST | Devanāgarī | IAST |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| क | ka | ख | kha | ग | ga | घ | gha | ङ | ṅa |
| च | ca | छ | cha | ज | ja | झ | jha | ञ | ña |
| ट | ṭa | ठ | ṭha | ड | ḍa | ढ | ḍha | ण | ṇa |
| त | ta | थ | tha | द | da | ध | dha | न | na |
| प | pa | फ | pha | ब | ba | भ | bha | म | ma |
| य | ya | र | ra | ल | la | व | va | |  |
| श | śa | ष | ṣa | स | sa | ह | ha | |  |

(All forms are shown with their inherent vowel a; the bare consonant value is obtained by mentally removing the final a, as if a virama were attached.)

## Anusvāra and Visarga as Marks

A vowel may be followed by the nasalization mark called **anusvāra** (ं, ṃ) or the voiceless aspiration mark called **visarga** (ः, ḥ) — for example, the final ः in रामः *rāmaḥ* ("Rāma [as subject]") is a visarga. Both are treated as independent consonantal segments phonologically, but graphically they are written as auxiliary marks attached to a vowel. For details, see [Anusvāra and Visarga](/en/sanskrit/script-and-phonology/anusvara-visarga/).
