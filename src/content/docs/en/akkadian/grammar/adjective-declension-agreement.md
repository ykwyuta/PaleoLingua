## Adjectives Agree with Their Noun

Akkadian adjectives agree with the noun they modify in **gender (masculine/feminine), number (singular/plural), and case (nominative/genitive/accusative)**. In other words, an adjective is itself a declinable word class that takes case endings just like a noun — it is not an invariable word simply placed next to the noun, as in Japanese.

## Singular Declension: Nearly the Same as Nouns

In the singular, adjective case endings largely match noun case endings (nominative `-um`, genitive `-im`, accusative `-am`). Consider the phrase "the mighty king," combining `šarrum` ("king," a masculine noun) with the adjective `dannum` ("mighty, strong").

<p class="cuneiform">𒊭𒅈𒊒𒌝 𒁕𒀭𒉡𒌝</p>

```
šarrum   dannum
king-NOM   mighty-NOM(m.sg.)
"the mighty king"
```

`šarrum` 𒊭𒅈𒊒𒌝 ("king," nominative) and `dannum` 𒁕𒀭𒉡𒌝 ("mighty," nominative) both carry the same `-um` ending, making their agreement in gender, number, and case immediately visible. The word order noun-then-adjective is the basic pattern of the Akkadian noun phrase — the adjective directly follows the noun it modifies.

## The Plural Diverges from the Noun Pattern

While the singular shares nearly identical case endings between noun and adjective, **the plural is where adjective declension breaks from noun declension**. Masculine plural nominative nouns take `-ū`, but masculine plural adjectives typically take a distinct ending, `-ūtum` (thought to derive from the feminine plural, functioning here as a collective-type form).

| Word | Part of speech | Singular (nom.) | Plural (nom.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| šarrum "king" | noun | -um | -ū |
| dannum "mighty" | adjective | -um | -ūtum |

<p class="cuneiform">𒊭𒅈𒊒 𒁕𒀭𒉡𒌈</p>

```
šarrū    dannūtum
king-NOM(pl.)   mighty-NOM(m.pl.)
"the mighty kings"
```

Akkadian adjective declension is thus "similar but not identical" to noun declension, and this distinction is especially visible in the plural. Learners often assume, on the strength of the singular's resemblance, that the plural will match too — a natural but mistaken assumption worth flagging early.

## Attributive versus Predicative Use

Adjectives have an **attributive** use, directly modifying a noun as part of a noun phrase (as in "the mighty king" above), and a **predicative** use, functioning as the predicate of a sentence ("the king is mighty"). In Akkadian, these two uses take strikingly different forms.

- Attributive: as shown above, the adjective takes the same case ending as its noun and is built into the noun phrase (`šarrum dannum`, "the mighty king").
- Predicative: predicative adjectival meaning in Akkadian is normally expressed not by an ordinary declined adjective form but by the special [stative (permansive)](/en/akkadian/grammar/stative-permansive/) form. "The king is mighty," for instance, is expressed as `šarrum dan` (king + the stative form built on the root of `dannum`).

<p class="cuneiform">𒊭𒅈𒊒𒌝 𒆗</p>

```
šarrum   dan
king-NOM   mighty(stative, 3ms)
"the king is mighty"
```

The same root that yields the declined adjective `dannum` in attributive position thus yields the stative form `dan` in predicative position. The full paradigm and grammatical character of the stative are treated in [The Stative (Permansive)](/en/akkadian/grammar/stative-permansive/).
