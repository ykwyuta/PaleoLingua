## The Archives of Ḫattuša

Nearly all surviving Hittite literature comes from the tens of thousands of clay tablet fragments recovered from the ruins of the royal palace at the capital, Ḫattuša (modern Boğazköy/Boğazkale). These were discovered through excavations beginning in the early 20th century, and appear to have been kept in orderly fashion across several archives within the palace complex. Because these were administrative archives, the surviving corpus is heavily weighted toward **practical documents tied to running the state and religious ritual texts**, rather than personal poetry or creative literature.

## Major Genres

| Genre | Content | Representative example |
|---|---|---|
| Annals/historical texts | Documents recording a king's deeds, chronologically or by topic | [The Anitta Text](/en/hittite/readings/anitta-text-excerpt/) |
| Edicts/instructions | Political proclamations a king issued to successors or officials | [The Proclamation of Telepinu](/en/hittite/readings/telepinu-proclamation-excerpt/) |
| Law codes | Legal documents listing specific rules on crime and compensation | [The Hittite Laws](/en/hittite/readings/hittite-laws-excerpt/) |
| Treaties | Political agreements with vassal states or peer great powers | [Treaty Formulas](/en/hittite/readings/kbo-treaty-formula/), [The Treaty of Kadesh](/en/hittite/readings/treaty-of-kadesh-bilingual/) |
| Prayers | Religious texts of petition, apology, or thanks to the gods | [The Plague Prayers of Muršili II](/en/hittite/readings/plague-prayers-mursili/), [The Prayer of Queen Puduḫepa](/en/hittite/readings/queen-puduhepa-prayer/) |
| Myths | Religious narrative literature about the gods | [The Illuyanka Myth](/en/hittite/readings/illuyanka-myth-excerpt/) |
| Royal ritual/titulary | Formulaic texts used at accession or state ceremonies | [Royal Titulary Formulas](/en/hittite/readings/royal-titulary-formula/) |

## A Literature of Borrowing and Translation

Much of Hittite mythological literature consists of translations or adaptations of stories originally from Hattic (the non-Indo-European language of Anatolia's indigenous population) or Hurrian (a language of northern Mesopotamia and Syria). The Illuyanka myth is one such example, thought to have originally been a narrative accompanying a Hattic ritual. In this sense, Hittite mythological literature is a valuable body of evidence for how a multiethnic, multilingual empire actively absorbed the traditions of surrounding cultures and wove them into its own religious ceremonies.

## Why So Many Administrative Documents?

The bias toward administrative and religious documents reflects the fact that these archives were, first and foremost, practical repositories supporting the state's administration and religious life. While some personal letters and everyday records have also been found, annals, treaties, and ritual texts dominate the corpus by sheer volume. As a result, the study of Hittite literature tends to be less a matter of analyzing pure literary works and more an **interdisciplinary undertaking closely tied to history, religious history, and legal history**.
