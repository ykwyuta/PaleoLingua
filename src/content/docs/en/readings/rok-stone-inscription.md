## What is the Rök stone?

The **Rök stone** (Old Norse/Swedish *Rökstenen*) stands at Rök church in Östergötland, southeastern Sweden, and is generally dated to the early 9th century (around 800 CE). Carved densely over its entire surface with more than 800 characters, it is **the longest known runic inscription**. Its opening lines tell us it was raised by a father, Varinn, in memory of his dead son, Vámóðr.

The inscription is written mainly in the Younger Futhark but also incorporates cryptic "cipher runes." Its latter portion contains difficult material that has been read as allusions to heroic legend, riddles, and even astronomical events; scholarly interpretation remains actively contested, and no fully settled consensus exists.

## The opening line: a memorial formula

Let's look at the opening of the inscription, where interpretation is relatively well agreed upon.

```
Aft Vámóð  standa  rúnar þær.
En Varinn  fáði,   faðir, aft faigian sunu.
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Word | Aft | Vámóð | standa | rúnar | þær |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning | in memory of | Vámóðr(ACC) | stand | runes(NOM) | these |

| Word | En | Varinn | fáði | faðir | aft faigian sunu |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning | and | Varinn(NOM) | colored/carved | the father | in memory of [his] doomed son |

**Translation**: "In memory of Vámóðr stand these runes. And Varinn, the father, carved [them] in memory of [his] son, fated to die."

## Grammar and vocabulary notes

- `standa` ("stand") is a verb that appears constantly in runic inscriptions to describe a memorial stone as "standing" — the same formula appears in "[Viking Age Runestones](/en/readings/viking-age-runestones/)."
- `fáði` ("colored," "carved") is the past tense of the verb *fá* ("to paint," "to inscribe"). Runic inscriptions were originally colored, the carved grooves filled with red or black pigment, and this verb directly reflects that act of production.
- `faigian` ("doomed," "fated to die") is the accusative weak form of the adjective *feigr* ("near death," "fated to die"). It's a more poetically loaded word than simply "dead" — it carries the sense of a death already fated.

## The rest of the inscription: an unsolved puzzle

Beyond the opening lines, the inscription is marked by allusions to legendary kings and heroes, interwoven with a recurring riddle-like formula, "tell what (*hvat er þat...*, "what is it...")." Some recent scholarship has proposed that parts of the text refer to a 9th-century cold spell or a solar eclipse, but these remain hypotheses rather than settled fact — the Rök stone is still regarded as one of the great unsolved puzzles of Scandinavian runology.
