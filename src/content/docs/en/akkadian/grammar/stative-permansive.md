## What Is the Stative (Permansive)?

Alongside the finite, tense-prefixed forms treated in [G-Stem Conjugation](/en/akkadian/grammar/g-stem-conjugation/) — the preterite (`iprus`), the durative/present (`iparras`), the perfect (`iptaras`) — Akkadian has a distinct form called the **stative** (also called the **permansive**). Built on the root `PRS` ("to decide, cut"), the stative takes the shape `paris`. Morphologically it stands apart from the other finite forms because person affixes attach directly to the bare root pattern, with no tense-marking prefix at all.

Where a form like `iprus` ("he decided") consists of a tense/person prefix (`i-`) plus the root plus (where relevant) a suffix, the stative `paris` consists of nothing more than the root's own vowel pattern (PaRiS) with a person affix attached directly. Because of this structural difference, the stative is understood not as expressing a tense (past/present/future) at all, but rather the **state the subject currently occupies**.

## The Stative Paradigm

Here is the stative of the root `PRS` ("to decide") across several persons. The 3ms form `paris` can mean "he is decided (a decision concerning him has been made)" or, depending on context, "he is decisive (a decider)."

| Person | Stative form | Cuneiform | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1cs | parsāku | 𒉺𒅈𒊓𒆪 | I am decided |
| 2ms | parsāta | 𒉺𒅈𒊓𒋫 | you (m.) are decided |
| 3ms | paris | 𒉺𒊑𒄑 | he is decided |
| 3fs | parsat | 𒉺𒅈𒊓𒀜 | she is decided |
| 3mp | parsū | 𒉺𒅈𒋢 | they (m.) are decided |
| 3fp | parsā | 𒉺𒅈𒊓 | they (f.) are decided |

<p class="cuneiform">𒉺𒊑𒄑</p>

```
pa-ri-is
decide(root PRS)-STAT.3ms
"he is decided / it has been decided"
```

<p class="cuneiform">𒉺𒅈𒊓𒆪</p>

```
pa-ar-sa-ku
decide(root PRS)-STAT.1cs
"I am decided"
```

Note that only the 3ms form `paris` (PaRiS) preserves the vowel between all three root consonants intact. In every other person, the second vowel syncopates, leaving the stem `pars-` (PaRS-), to which the person affix (`-āku`, `-āta`, `-at`, `-ū`, `-ā`, etc.) attaches.

## Ties to Nominal/Adjectival Predication and Passive/Resultative Meaning

Although morphologically the stative is one member of the verbal paradigm, semantically it behaves much more like a **nominal or adjectival predicate**. As seen in [Adjective Declension and Agreement](/en/akkadian/grammar/adjective-declension-agreement/), a predicative adjective ("the king is mighty") is expressed not by an ordinary declined adjective but by a stative form (`dan`, "he is mighty"). The stative can thus be built on either a verbal root or an adjectival root, and in either case its function is the same: to state the condition the subject currently occupies.

Furthermore, the stative of a transitive verbal root frequently carries a **passive or resultative meaning**. `paris` (from `PRS`, "to decide") is a textbook case: rather than describing the active past event "he decided," it describes the **present state resulting from that action** — "he is decided / the decision has been made." This quality — expressing the present state left behind by an action, rather than the active event itself — sets the stative apart fundamentally from the tense-prefixed finite forms like the preterite `iprus` or the durative `iparras`; the full picture of those tense-prefixed forms is treated in [G-Stem Conjugation](/en/akkadian/grammar/g-stem-conjugation/).
