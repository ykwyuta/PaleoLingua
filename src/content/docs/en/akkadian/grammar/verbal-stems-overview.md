## The Idea of a Verbal Stem

Akkadian verbs systematically shift voice (active, passive, and so on) and the nature of the action (a simple act, an intensified or repeated one, a caused one) by applying different "stem" patterns to the same root. This is a larger-scale application of the same "same root, different pattern" principle seen in [The Triconsonantal Root System](/en/akkadian/grammar/triconsonantal-root-system/). There are four basic stems.

| Stem | Common name | Formal marker | Basic function |
|---|---|---|---|
| G | Grundstamm ("basic stem") | No affix or doubling | The root's plain, unmarked action |
| D | Doubled-stem | Doubling of the root's second (middle) radical | Intensive/factitive — often "to make X happen thoroughly," or transitivizing |
| Š | Causative stem | š- prefix | "to cause/make [someone] do X" |
| N | Passive/reflexive stem | n- prefix | Passive, middle, or spontaneous action |

## Meaning Shifts Across the Four Stems on √PRS

Comparing the third-person masculine singular preterite of the root `PRS` "to cut, separate, decide" across all four stems shows clearly how the same root's meaning is extended stem by stem.

| Stem | Transliteration | Cuneiform | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G | iprus | 𒅁𒊒𒊻 | i- (3ms) + prus (G preterite) | "he decided / cut apart" |
| D | uparris | 𒌋𒉺𒅈𒊑𒄑 | u- (3ms) + parris (D preterite, doubled r) | "he settled (a matter) decisively / adjudicated" |
| Š | ušapris | 𒌋𒊭𒀊𒊑𒄑 | u- (3ms) + šapris (Š preterite, š- causative marker) | "he caused (someone/something) to be separated" |
| N | ipparis | 𒅁𒉺𒊑𒄑 | i- (3ms) + pparis (N preterite, n- assimilated) | "it was cut apart / decided" |

Against G-stem `iprus`'s plain sense of "to cut apart, decide," D-stem `uparris` emphasizes the action or foregrounds its result ("to settle decisively"). Š-stem `ušapris` adds a causative sense — "to make someone/something undergo that action" — while N-stem `ipparis` presents the same action from the viewpoint of the entity it affects, as a passive or spontaneous event. Note also that the N-stem's initial `n-` assimilates to the following consonant, which is why it is written `ipparis` (from underlying *inparis) rather than *inparis.

## The t-Infixed Variants (Gt, Dt, Št, Ntn)

Each of the four basic stems above has a further derived form with a `-t-` infixed immediately before the stem vowel. These add nuances of reflexivity, reciprocity, or repetition on top of the basic stem.

| Derived stem | Based on | Typical nuance |
|---|---|---|
| Gt | G | Reflexive/reciprocal ("to do X to one another"), or, for some roots, simply a distinct lexical variant |
| Dt | D | Passive of the D-stem; occasionally reflexive |
| Št | Š | Reflexive of the causative ("to cause oneself to do X"), or a passive use of the Š-stem |
| Ntn | N | Iterative/durative (with a further doubled radical, "to be done repeatedly") |

The t-infixed variants are used far less often than the four basic stems, but for individual roots they often develop their own idiomatic meanings, so it is always worth checking a specific attestation against a dictionary when reading real texts.

## Summary: Stem-Meaning Correspondences

| Stem | Voice/nature | In a phrase |
|---|---|---|
| G | Active, basic | "to do X" |
| D | Active, intensive/result-focused | "to do X thoroughly/decisively" |
| Š | Active, causative | "to cause X" |
| N | Passive/reflexive/spontaneous | "to be X'd / to become X on its own" |
| Gt/Dt/Št/Ntn | Reflexive, reciprocal, or iterative variants of each stem | "to do X to one another / to oneself / repeatedly" |
