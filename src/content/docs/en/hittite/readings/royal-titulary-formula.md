## From Personal Name to Title: Tabarna and Tawananna

Hittite official documents, treaties, and inscriptions typically open with a series of formulaic titles attached to the king's name. Foremost among them is **Tabarna** (also spelled Labarna). This word was originally the personal name of **Labarna**, held to be the first king of the Hittite Old Kingdom, but in later usage it became fixed as a **title** referring to Hittite kingship in general rather than any specific individual. Similarly, the queen's title **Tawananna** is thought to have originated as the personal name of a particular queen before shifting into a general title for later queens. This resembles how the Egyptian "Pharaoh" or the Roman "Caesar" each began as a personal name before becoming a general title.

## The Titles "Great King" and "Hero"

Following Tabarna, a king's titulary often combines the following elements.

```
LUGAL.GAL LUGAL KUR URUḪa-at-ti UR.SAG
king.great king land Ḫatti hero
"Great King, King of the Land of Ḫatti, Hero"
```

**LUGAL.GAL** "Great King" is a diplomatic title placing its bearer on equal footing with the kings of comparable great powers (Egypt, Assyria, Babylonia, and the like), and was especially emphasized in treaties between peer great powers, such as the [Treaty of Kadesh](/en/hittite/readings/treaty-of-kadesh-bilingual/). **UR.SAG** "hero" is a title honoring the king's martial prowess, appearing in the preambles of many inscriptions and treaties.

## The Self-Designation "My Sun"

A striking self-designation the Hittite king used for himself is **DUTU-ŠI**, an Akkadogram meaning "My Sun."

```
DUTU-ŠI-ma-za a-pu-u-un me-mi-an ḫa-an-da-a-iz-zi
My.Sun(royal self-ref.)-but-(reflexive) that word-ACC keep-3SG.PRES
"but My Sun (the king) keeps that word (promise)"
```

This use of "sun" as a royal title echoes the way the supreme goddess was called "the Sun-goddess of Arinna," as seen in [The Prayer of Queen Puduḫepa](/en/hittite/readings/queen-puduhepa-prayer/), and forms one of the central motifs of Hittite royal ideology, linking kingship to sun-god worship. It is comparable to the belief that the Egyptian pharaoh was an incarnation of the sun-god Ra — one example of a link between kingship and the sun found broadly across the ancient Near East and North Africa.

## The Function of the Titulary Formula

This string of titles was not mere flowery language; it served the practical function of establishing the speaker's (the king's) legitimacy and authority at the head of a document. In treaty documents especially, the particular wording of a titulary could implicitly signal the power relationship with the other party (a peer great power, or a subordinate ally), meaning the choice of titles itself carried diplomatic weight.
