## What are suffix pronouns?

Old Egyptian has a set of pronouns that attach directly to nouns, prepositions, and verbs to mark person ("I," "you," "he," etc.). These are called **suffix pronouns**. They are not free-standing words but always attach to a preceding word, and are conventionally written with an equals sign (=) separating them from the host word.

## The full paradigm

| Person | Suffix | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | =j | "-i" |
| 2nd sg. masc. | =k | "-k" |
| 2nd sg. fem. | =ṯ | "-tch" |
| 3rd sg. masc. | =f | "-ef" |
| 3rd sg. fem. | =s | "-es" |
| 1st pl. | =n | "-en" |
| 2nd pl. | =ṯn | "-tchen" |
| 3rd pl. | =sn | "-sen" |

(There are also 2nd/3rd person dual forms, =ṯny and =sny, though they are relatively rare. For now, the eight singular and plural forms above are the ones to master.)

All the consonants needed for these suffixes (j, k, ṯ, f, s, n, ṯn, sn) are present among the Table 1 uniliteral signs, so every suffix pronoun can be spelled out phonetically.

## Use 1: attached to nouns, marking possession

Attached directly to a noun, a suffix pronoun creates a possessive expression like "my" or "his." Here it is with **pr** "house."

<p class="hieroglyph">𓊪𓂋𓆑</p>

```
pr=f
house=he/his
"his house"
```

<p class="hieroglyph">𓊪𓂋𓇋</p>

```
pr=j
house=I/my
"my house"
```

## Use 2: attached to prepositions

Suffix pronouns also attach to prepositions to create expressions like "to him" or "for him." Here is the dative/directional preposition **n** "to, for."

<p class="hieroglyph">𓈖𓆑</p>

```
n=f
to=he/him
"to him, for him"
```

## Use 3: attached to verbs, marking the subject

One of the most important uses of suffix pronouns is attaching directly to a verb to mark its **subject**. Here is the verb root **sḏm** "hear."

<p class="hieroglyph">𓋴𓆓𓅓𓆑</p>

```
sḏm=f
hear=he
"he hears" (verb root sḏm + subject-marking suffix pronoun =f)
```

This exact form, sḏm=f, is so central to Egyptian grammar that Egyptologists use it as the standard name for an entire family of verb forms — see the dedicated article [The sḏm=f Form: The Basic Egyptian Verb Form](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/sdm-f-verb-form/). For now, the key takeaway is that suffix pronouns serve as the subject marker on a verb.
