## Background: The Genre of Private Legal Documents

Among all surviving Akkadian texts, one of the largest categories by sheer volume is the **private legal document**. Tablets recording individual legal transactions — the sale of a house or field, adoption, marriage contracts, loans of silver or grain — survive in enormous numbers from every period and region, from Old Babylonian times down to the Neo-Babylonian era.

These documents were not casual private notes but were drawn up as formal **records of transaction** carrying legal force. Beyond simply recording the terms of a deal, they typically close with a **list of witnesses**, and were often further validated by the **seal impressions** of the parties and witnesses. Both the witness list and the seals served the same purpose: proving that a transaction had taken place, in case of a later dispute.

## The Standard Structure of a Sale Contract

A typical sale contract generally contains the following elements, in this order:

| Component | Content |
|---|---|
| Description of the object | Details of the house, field, slave, etc. being sold (size, location, adjoining properties, etc.) |
| Transaction clause | The core sentence: "X bought Y from Z for N shekels of silver" |
| Parties | The names of the seller and buyer |
| Witness list | A list of "witness: ..." entries, each introduced by the cuneiform heading IGI |
| Date | A date formula — a year-name in Old Babylonian, or the name of the eponym official (līmu) in Assyrian texts |

## The Core Sale Clause

The heart of a sale contract is usually a clause of the form "bought X for silver." Below is a sentence constructed to show the typical grammar and vocabulary of this clause (composed to reflect a formula widely attested in Old Babylonian sale contracts, rather than a verbatim quotation of one specific numbered tablet).

<p class="cuneiform">𒁉𒋫𒄠 𒀀𒈾 𒅗𒊍𒉿𒅎 𒄿𒊭𒄠</p>

```
bi-ta-am    a-na    ka-as-pi-im    i-ša-am
house-ACC   to/for  silver-GEN     buy-PRET.3MS
```

"He bought the house for silver" — *bītam*, the accusative (direct-object) form of *bītum* "house," is the object of the verb *īšām*, the third-person masculine singular preterite of *šâmum* "to buy." *ana kaspim* "for silver" is a prepositional phrase indicating the price paid. In an actual document, the seller's name and a fuller description of the object would normally precede this core clause, and the buyer's name would follow.

## The Witness-List Formula

After the sale clause, the contract moves into a section listing witnesses. This part repeats the cuneiform heading **IGI** (literally the Sumerian word for "eye," but used in Akkadian legal documents as a fixed heading meaning "witness:") before each witness's name.

```
IGI PN₁    IGI PN₂    IGI PN₃ ...
witness: PN1   witness: PN2   witness: PN3 ...
```

Here **PN** ("persona nomen," i.e. "personal name") is a conventional Assyriological abbreviation standing in for a spot where an actual document would give a specific individual's name. Real contracts typically list anywhere from three to ten witnesses, and the scribe who drew up the document often adds his own name near the end of the list.

## Vocabulary Breakdown

| Transliteration | Cuneiform | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| bi-ta-am | 𒁉𒋫𒄠 | bītam (bīt- + accusative -am) | the house (obj.) |
| a-na | 𒀀𒈾 | ana (preposition) | to, for |
| ka-as-pi-im | 𒅗𒊍𒉿𒅎 | kaspim (kasp- + genitive -im) | of silver |
| i-ša-am | 𒄿𒊭𒄠 | 3ms preterite of šâmum ("to buy") | he bought |
| IGI | 𒅆 | witness-list heading (Sumerogram) | witness: |

## The Source Value of This Genre

Unlike the ideologically charged formal documents of royal inscriptions or law codes, private legal documents are highly empirical sources recording actual individual transactions as they happened. Land prices, the names and social standing of the parties involved, and the web of relationships revealed by who appears as a witness on which document — all of this makes private contracts a primary source of particular importance for economic and social historians reconstructing the concrete details of ancient economic life and social structure.
