## Basic structure: prefixes + root + suffixes

The Sumerian finite verb is famously "polysynthetic," packing a large amount of grammatical information into a single word. Its basic skeleton looks like this:

```
(chain of prefixes) - root - (suffixes)
```

The prefix slots hold, among other things, markers of mood, elements indicating direction or manner, and markers of person (who is doing what to whom), all stacked in a fixed order. The suffix slots hold markers for the person and number of the absolutive argument (roughly, the subject of an intransitive verb or the object of a transitive one).

This system is genuinely complex, and specialists still debate parts of its analysis. This article aims only to establish the big picture — a verb consists of a prefix chain, a root, and suffixes — without going into the finer rules.

## The prefix mu-

Among the many possible prefixes, `mu-` is especially frequent. It is often described as "ventive," marking motion or action directed "hither," toward the speaker, and it commonly appears in clauses describing a concrete, completed event.

```
lugal-e   e2   mu-du3
king-ERG  house VENT-he.built
"The king built a house."
```

In `mu-du3`, the prefix `mu-` combines with the root `du3` ("to build").

## Person suffixes marking the absolutive argument (overview)

A suffix at the end of the verb can mark the person and number of the absolutive argument (the intransitive subject or transitive object). Common examples include `-en` for 1st person singular ("I") and `-en` for 2nd person singular ("you," distinguished from 1st person by context). Third person singular is very often left unmarked.

## How to keep learning

The full verbal conjugation system is one of the more advanced topics in Sumerian grammar. A good approach at this stage is to memorize set example sentences like `lugal-e e2 mu-du3` as whole units, then move on to [Marû and Ḫamṭu verb stems](/en/sumerian/grammar/maru-hamtu-aspect/) to build up your understanding of the verb system gradually.
