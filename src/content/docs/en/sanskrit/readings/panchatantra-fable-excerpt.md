## What Is the Pañcatantra?

The **Pañcatantra** ("Five Treatises/Books") is a work of didactic literature that embeds animal fables within a frame narrative to teach princes practical worldly wisdom (*nīti*). According to tradition, the sage **Viṣṇuśarman** composed it as teaching material to turn three foolish, indifferent-to-learning princes of a certain king into capable rulers within just six months. Its date and original form remain unsettled, but the oldest surviving layer is often placed somewhere between the turn of the era and around 300 CE. The whole is organized into five books (*tantras*): **Mitra-bheda** (the loss of friends), **Mitra-prāpti** (the gaining of friends), **Kākolūkīyam** (the story of crows and owls — war and peace), **Labdhapraṇāśa** (losing what one has gained), and **Aparīkṣitakāraka** (rash action).

## The Text: A Verse on Learning and Practice from the Introduction

The frame story that introduces the Pañcatantra contains the following famous verse, characteristic of the work's practical, worldly wisdom.

```
अजरामरवत् प्राज्ञो विद्यामर्थं च चिन्तयेत् ।
गृहीत इव केशेषु मृत्युना धर्ममाचरेत् ॥

ajarāmaravat prājño vidyām arthaṃ ca cintayet |
gṛhīta iva keśeṣu mṛtyunā dharmam ācaret ||
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Devanāgarī | IAST | Morphological analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| अजरामरवत् | ajarāmaravat | a- (negative) + jara "old age" + amara "immortal" + -vat ("as if") | as if never to age or die |
| प्राज्ञः | prājñaḥ | prājña "wise (person)," nom. sg. | the wise one |
| विद्याम् | vidyām | vidyā "learning/knowledge," acc. sg. | learning (acc.) |
| अर्थम् | artham | artha "wealth/practical gain," acc. sg. | wealth (acc.) |
| च | ca | conjunction | and |
| चिन्तयेत् | cintayet | √cint "to consider/pursue," 3sg. optative | should pursue |
| गृहीतः इव | gṛhītaḥ iva | gṛhīta "seized/grasped" + iva "as if," nom. sg. | as if seized |
| केशेषु | keśeṣu | keśa "hair," locative pl. | by the hair |
| मृत्युना | mṛtyunā | mṛtyu "death," instrumental sg. | by Death |
| धर्मम् | dharmam | dharma "duty/righteousness," acc. sg. | duty/dharma (acc.) |
| आचरेत् | ācaret | ā-√car "to practice," 3sg. optative | should practice |

**Translation:** "A wise person should pursue learning and wealth as if [they would] never grow old or die; but should practice duty (dharma) as if seized by Death by the hair."

## Uniting Two Seemingly Contradictory Attitudes

The cleverness of this verse lies in the way it unites **two apparently contradictory senses of urgency** within a single piece of life advice. Regarding learning (*vidyā*) and material gain (*artha*), it counsels patient, unhurried pursuit — "as if never to age or die." But regarding the practice of duty and righteousness (*dharma*), it counsels the opposite: act as though Death has already seized you by the hair, with no time to spare. This can be read as practical wisdom distinguishing, among the four traditional Hindu goals of life (*puruṣārthas*: *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* "desire," and *mokṣa* "liberation"), those things best cultivated slowly over time from those that demand urgent, immediate practice.

## Influence on World Literature

The Pañcatantra is known not only for its many retellings within India (the Hitopadeśa being one) but for an extraordinarily wide transmission across world literature. Around the 6th century CE, the Sasanian Persian physician Burzōē translated the Sanskrit original (or an intermediate adaptation of it) into Middle Persian (Pahlavi); this was subsequently translated into Arabic, becoming known as **Kalīla wa Dimna**. Kalīla wa Dimna was then translated in turn into Syriac, Greek, Hebrew, Latin, and eventually a series of medieval European languages, and is thought to have ultimately influenced European fable literature — an indirect influence has even been suggested on La Fontaine's fables. The genre of animal fable itself is frequently compared typologically to the Greek **Aesopic fables**, but scholars remain divided on any direct line of influence between the two traditions, and the question should be treated with caution.
