## Small Words That Gather at "Second Position"

Hittite sentences contain many small, unaccented words — enclitics — that cannot stand independently on their own. These enclitics attach, in a fixed order, to the first accented element in the clause, which is usually the clause-initial connective particle. This phenomenon is frequently cited in Hittite studies as a classic example of "Wackernagel's Law," the tendency in many old Indo-European languages for enclitics to cluster in the sentence's second position.

## The Order of the Enclitic Chain

The Hittite enclitic chain generally follows a fixed order along these lines:

| Slot | Type of element | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clause-initial connective (nu, ta, šu) | nu- "and" |
| 2 | Quotative particle -wa(r)- (marks direct speech) | -wa- |
| 3 | Dative/accusative personal pronoun enclitic | -mu "to me," -šši "to him/her," -at "it" |
| 4 | Reflexive particle -za | -za |
| 5 | Local/aspectual particles (-kan, -ašta, -šan, etc.) | -kan |

## A Concrete Example

```
nu-mu-kan
and-to.me-(local particle)
"and to me (something happened)"

n-an-kan
and-it-(local particle)
"and it (was done)"
```

As these examples show, the personal pronoun enclitics (-mu "to me," -an "it") and the local particle -kan, which marks location or a phase of the action, attach to the clause-initial connective nu in a fixed order. None of these enclitics can stand on their own; they always appear "riding on" the leading element, most often the clause-initial connective.

## Why Did This System Develop?

This enclitic chain is thought to be organized less around the sentence's syntactic structure per se than around its **prosodic grouping** in speech. It is helpful to think of the clause-initial connective not so much as carrying independent meaning, but as functioning as a "platform" that anchors the following string of enclitics to the front of the clause. Similar phenomena are found in other old Indo-European languages, such as Ancient Greek and Vedic Sanskrit, and are thought to reflect an old feature of Proto-Indo-European syntax.

## The Importance of Enclitic Order

The order of the enclitics is fundamentally fixed and cannot be freely rearranged by the speaker. Understanding this order is therefore essential background for correctly parsing a Hittite sentence. The article on [The Sentence-Initial Particles Nu, Ta, and Šu](/en/hittite/grammar/sentence-initial-particles-nu-ta-su/) goes into more detail on the connectives that anchor this chain.
