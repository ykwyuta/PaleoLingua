## What is a determinative?

A determinative is an unpronounced sign placed at the **end** of a word to indicate its semantic category. Because Egyptian doesn't write vowels, words spelled purely phonetically can share the same consonant skeleton while meaning entirely different things. Determinatives help the reader instantly narrow down which meaning is intended.

## Common determinatives

| Determinative | Hieroglyph | Gardiner code | Category |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking legs | 𓂻 | D54 | Motion, verbs of "going" |
| Seated man | 𓀀 | A1 | People, male occupations |
| Seated woman | 𓁐 | B1 | People, female occupations |
| Child with sidelock | 𓀔 | A17 | Children |
| Papyrus roll | 𓏛 | Y1 | Abstract concepts, writing |
| God on a standard | 𓅆 | G7 | Deities (especially Horus) |
| Plural strokes | 𓏥 | Z2 | Plural |

## Example: verbs of motion

Verbs of motion — "go out," "depart," and similar — frequently take the walking-legs determinative 𓂻 (Gardiner D54). Even though the verb's root itself is not visually related to motion, the determinative tells the reader "this word concerns an action of movement."

<p class="hieroglyph">𓊪𓂋𓇋𓂻</p>

```
prj
go out, emerge (root pr + j, plus the motion determinative)
```

## Example: markers for people

Words for occupations or social roles frequently take the seated-man determinative (𓀀) or seated-woman determinative (𓁐), depending on gender. Words for children take the determinative depicting a child with a sidelock of hair (𓀔). Names of gods and divine beings often take the determinative of a falcon perched on a standard (𓅆), most closely associated with the god Horus.

## Example: abstract concepts and the plural

Words for intangible, abstract concepts — thoughts, emotions, actions — frequently take the papyrus-roll determinative 𓏛 (Y1), thought to symbolize "something that is written down." To mark a word as plural, scribes used the plural strokes 𓏥 (Z2), three short parallel lines. This is sometimes treated as a kind of determinative, and sometimes as its own independent grammatical marker.

## Determinatives are not read aloud

Because determinatives are unpronounced, they can simply be ignored when reading Egyptian aloud. Think of them as a silent "label" a scribe attached purely to help the reader.
