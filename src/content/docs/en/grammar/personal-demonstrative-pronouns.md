## Independent personal pronouns

Alongside the person markers that attach to the verb, Sumerian also has independent personal pronouns that can stand on their own. The best-attested and most secure are the first and second person singular forms.

| Person | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1st singular | ĝe26-e | "I" |
| 2nd singular | za-e | "you" |

```
ĝe26-e   lugal-me-en
I        king-COP-1SG
"I am king."
```

Third-person and plural pronoun forms are also discussed in the literature, but the evidence for them is sparser and their analysis varies more between grammarians, so this article sticks to the well-established 1st/2nd person singular forms.

## The demonstrative/anaphoric element bi

The word that most closely corresponds to "that" or "it" in Sumerian is `bi`. It attaches after a noun and does double duty: as a straightforward demonstrative ("that ___") and as an anaphoric reference back to something already mentioned ("its ___").

```
e2-bi
house-that/its
"that house" / "its house"
```

## Why the pronoun system looks sparse

Sumerian's pronoun system overlaps considerably in function with the verb's person markers (see [Personal Affixes and Basic Verb Structure](/en/grammar/personal-affixes-verb-basics/)), so independent pronouns are used far less often than in English or Japanese. In most contexts, the affixes attached to the verb or noun already make clear who is doing what, without needing a separate pronoun.
