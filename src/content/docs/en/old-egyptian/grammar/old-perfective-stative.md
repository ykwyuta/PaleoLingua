## A verb form for states, not actions

Alongside action-denoting verb forms like the [sḏm=f form](/en/old-egyptian/grammar/sdm-f-verb-form/), Old Egyptian has a special verb form that marks the **resulting state** of its subject. Egyptologists call this the **Old Perfective** or the **Stative**.

Where sḏm=f "he hears" describes the action itself, the Old Perfective/Stative expresses "he is/was in a state of..." — the state resulting from an action. Roughly, it corresponds to English glosses like "he is satisfied" or "he is at peace."

## An example with ḥtp

Let's look at the Stative using the root **ḥtp** "offering, be satisfied, be at peace."

<p class="hieroglyph">𓎛𓏏𓊪</p>

```
ḥtp
"offering, be satisfied, be at peace" (root)
```

Adding a person ending produces a Stative form meaning roughly "he is at peace." For 3rd person singular masculine, the form is generally cited as something like **ḥtp=w**, or in a form close to unmarked (the precise pronunciation and vowel pattern are difficult to reconstruct with certainty; what follows illustrates the general shape rather than a definitively fixed spelling).

<p class="hieroglyph">𓎛𓏏𓊪𓅱</p>

```
ḥtp=w
be-at-peace-3sg.masc.ending
"he is in a state of being at peace" (illustrating the Stative/Old Perfective pattern)
```

## The person-ending paradigm (commonly cited forms)

The Stative uses its own set of person endings, distinct from the sḏm=f suffix pronouns. The following are commonly cited in standard teaching grammars (not every cell of the full paradigm is equally certain; the table below shows four representative persons, and the paradigm continues beyond these).

| Person | Ending |
|---|---|
| 1st sg. | -kwj |
| 3rd sg. masc. | -w (or close to unmarked) |
| 3rd sg. fem. | -tj |
| 3rd pl. | -w |

## A typological parallel to the Semitic stative

The Old Perfective/Stative is functionally comparable to the Semitic "stative" verb category and to Akkadian's **permansive** (a suffix conjugation expressing a resulting state rather than an action). The very idea of a dedicated verb category for states, as opposed to actions, is considered one of the shared features linking Egyptian and the Semitic languages within the Afroasiatic family.
