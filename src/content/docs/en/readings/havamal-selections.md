## What is the Hávamál?

The *Hávamál* ("Sayings of the High One" — "the High One" being one of Óðinn's epithets) is one of the poems of the Poetic Edda, presenting worldly wisdom, proverbs, and magical lore in Óðinn's own voice. It is generally thought to be a compilation of several originally distinct poetic traditions rather than a single unified composition. It's also an ideal text for seeing the alliterative verse discussed in "[Introduction to Eddic Poetry](/en/readings/eddic-poetry-intro/)" in action.

## Stanza 1: advice for a guest

The poem's opening stanza gives advice for a traveler entering someone else's house.

```
Gáttir allar,       áðr gangi fram,
um skoðask skyli,   um skyggnast skyli,
því at óvíst er at vita,
hvar óvinir sitja    á fleti fyrir.
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Word | Gáttir | allar | áðr | gangi | fram | um skoðask skyli |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning | doorways | all | before | (one) go(SUBJ) | forward | one should look around |

**Translation**: "All doorways, before stepping forward, must be looked around, must be peered into — for it is impossible to know where enemies may be sitting, waiting, on the bench ahead."

### Grammar notes

- `skyli` is the present subjunctive of *skulu* ("shall," an obligation modal), a form that appears constantly in this poem's advice-giving stanzas.
- `óvíst er at vita` ("it is uncertain to know") is an impersonal construction, with no overt subject for `er` ("is").
- `sitja fyrir` is an idiom meaning "to sit ahead," i.e., "to lie in wait."

## Stanza 76: fame that never dies

One of the most widely quoted stanzas in the whole Hávamál, contrasting the transience of wealth and life with the permanence of reputation.

```
Deyr fé,       deyja frændr,
deyr sjalfr it sama,
en orðstírr    deyr aldregi
hveim er sér góðan getr.
```

**Word-by-word gloss**

| Deyr | fé | deyja | frændr | deyr | sjalfr | it sama |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dies | cattle/wealth | die | kinsmen | dies | oneself | the same |

| en | orðstírr | deyr | aldregi | hveim er sér góðan getr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| but | reputation | dies | never | for the one who wins a good [reputation] for himself |

**Translation**: "Cattle die, kinsmen die, one dies oneself the same [way]; but reputation never dies, for the one who has won himself a good one."

### Grammar notes

- `deyja` ("to die") is a Class VI strong verb; `deyr` is its 3rd-person singular present form.
- `orðstírr` ("fame," "renown") is a compound of *orð* ("word") and *stírr* ("brightness, gleam"), literally something like "word-gleam."
- `aldregi` ("never") is a negative adverb modifying the verb `deyr`.
- The `er` in `hveim er...` is an invariant relative particle ("who/that"), which combines with `hveim` (dative, "for whom") to give the meaning "for the one who..."
