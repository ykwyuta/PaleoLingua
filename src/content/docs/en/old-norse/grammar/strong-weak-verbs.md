## Two ways to form the past tense

Old Norse verbs split into two broad types based on how they form the past tense.

- **Weak verbs**: add a dental suffix (*-ð-, -d-, -t-*) to the stem (the *kalla* example in [The Basics of Verb Conjugation](/en/old-norse/grammar/old-norse-verb-basics/)).
- **Strong verbs**: no suffix at all — instead, the **stem vowel itself alternates** (a process called ablaut), exactly as in English *sing – sang – sung*.

Strong verbs make up a large part of the core Old Norse vocabulary and are traditionally sorted into seven classes based on their vowel-alternation pattern.

## The four principal parts

Dictionaries and grammars normally cite strong verbs by four "principal parts":

| Principal part | Description |
|---|---|
| Infinitive | dictionary form |
| Past singular | 1st/3rd person singular past |
| Past plural | past plural (also the stem for 2nd person singular past) |
| Past participle | the perfect participle form |

## Class I strong verb example: bíta ("to bite")

Class I (*í – ei – i – i*) has the clearest vowel-alternation pattern of all, so it makes a good first example.

| Principal part | Form | Vowel |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | bíta | í |
| Past singular | beit | ei |
| Past plural | bitu | i |
| Past participle | bitinn | i |

## Person inflection of bíta

| Person | Present sg. | Past sg. | Past pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | bít | beit | bitum |
| 2nd | bítr | beitt | bituð |
| 3rd | bítr | beit | bitu |

## Example sentence

```
hann  beit    orminn
hann  beit    orm-inn
he(NOM) bit  serpent-the(ACC)
"He bit the serpent."
```

## Why "strong" and "weak"?

The terms "strong" and "weak" for these two verb types come from the 19th-century grammarian Jacob Grimm. He called verbs that can form the past tense on the strength of the stem alone (with no outside suffix) "strong," and verbs that need the "external help" of a dental suffix "weak." Historically, strong verbs are the older layer, inheriting the ablaut system of Proto-Indo-European, while weak verbs are an innovation that developed within Germanic itself.
