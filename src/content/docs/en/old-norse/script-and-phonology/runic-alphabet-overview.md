## What are runes?

Runes are a phonetic writing system used to write the Germanic languages from roughly the 2nd century CE through the Middle Ages. The name **futhark** comes from the sound values of the first six letters of the sequence, *f, u, þ, a, r, k* — the same naming logic that gives "alphabet" its name from *alpha, beta*. The letterforms are angular, built almost entirely from straight and diagonal strokes, a shape usually explained as an adaptation to carving into wood and stone with a knife.

## Features of the writing system

- **A phonemic script**: each rune corresponds, in principle, to a single phoneme.
- **Angular letterforms**: curves are almost entirely absent, replaced by combinations of vertical and diagonal strokes — thought to avoid horizontal lines that would run along, and be lost in, the wood grain.
- **Both directions of writing occur**: early inscriptions can run left-to-right, right-to-left, or even alternate direction line by line (*boustrophedon*, "as the ox plows").
- **Word division**: dots or vertically stacked dots sometimes mark word boundaries, though this was never fully consistent.

## The Elder Futhark (c. 2nd–8th century)

The oldest attested runic system, consisting of 24 characters. It was used to write the "Common Germanic" of a period before North and West Germanic had fully diverged (often called Proto-Norse). The 24 runes are traditionally divided into three groups of eight called **ættir** ("families"), and the whole system takes its name, *fuþark*, from the first six letters of the first group.

| Group | Runes (selection) |
|---|---|
| 1st ætt | ᚠ f, ᚢ u, ᚦ þ, ᚨ a, ᚱ r, ᚲ k |
| 2nd ætt | ᚷ g, ᚹ w, ᚺ h, ᚾ n, ᛁ i, ᛃ j |
| 3rd ætt | ᛈ p, ᛇ z, ᛊ s, ᛏ t, ᛒ b, ᛖ e, ᛗ m, ᛚ l, ᛜ ŋ, ᛟ o, ᛞ d |

## The Younger Futhark (c. 8th–12th century)

It is this reduced, 16-rune system that recorded Viking Age Old Norse. Curiously, the spoken language's phoneme inventory had actually grown more complex over this period (more vowel qualities, for instance), even as the number of letters shrank from 24 to 16. The gap is closed by heavy **polyvalence**: a single rune stands for several phonemes. ᚢ (u), for instance, can represent /u, o, y, ø, w/, and ᛁ (i) can represent /i, e/. The Younger Futhark also has a simplified variant optimized for ease of carving, the **short-twig runes**.

| Rune | Name | Main sound value(s) |
|---|---|---|
| ᚠ | fé | f |
| ᚢ | úr | u, o, y, ø, w |
| ᚦ | þurs | þ, ð |
| ᚬ | áss | a, æ, o |
| ᚱ | reið | r |
| ᚴ | kaun | k, g |
| ᚼ | hagall | h |
| ᚾ | nauð | n |
| ᛁ | ís | i, e |
| ᛅ | ár | a |
| ᛋ | sól | s |
| ᛏ | týr | t, d |
| ᛒ | bjarkan | b, p |
| ᛘ | maðr | m |
| ᛚ | lögr | l |
| ᛦ | ýr | y, R |

The name and sound value of each rune is covered in more depth in the next article, "[Rune Names and Sound Values](/en/old-norse/script-and-phonology/rune-names-and-sounds/)."
