Pronouns use the same eight-case, three-number system as nouns, but their actual forms deviate sharply from the regular patterns seen in, for instance, [The Declension of A-Stem Masculine Nouns](/en/sanskrit/grammar/a-stem-masculine-declension/). This is common across Indo-European languages: pronouns, especially personal pronouns, are among the most basic and frequently used words, and high-frequency items tend to preserve irregular forms — worn down by sound change or blended from multiple original roots (suppletion) — more readily than the regular lexicon.

## The Personal Pronoun aham "I"

| Case | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | अहम् aham | आवाम् āvām | वयम् vayam |
| Accusative | माम् mām | आवाम् āvām | अस्मान् asmān |
| Instrumental | मया mayā | आवाभ्याम् āvābhyām | अस्माभिः asmābhiḥ |
| Dative | मह्यम् mahyam | आवाभ्याम् āvābhyām | अस्मभ्यम् asmabhyam |
| Ablative | मत् mat | आवाभ्याम् āvābhyām | अस्मत् asmat |
| Genitive | मम mama | आवयोः āvayoḥ | अस्माकम् asmākam |
| Locative | मयि mayi | आवयोः āvayoḥ | अस्मासु asmāsu |

The first-person pronoun makes no gender distinction whatsoever (the same forms serve male, female, or neuter speakers) — a significant contrast with nouns.

## The Personal Pronoun tvam "You"

| Case | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | त्वम् tvam | युवाम् yuvām | यूयम् yūyam |
| Accusative | त्वाम् tvām | युवाम् yuvām | युष्मान् yuṣmān |
| Instrumental | त्वया tvayā | युवाभ्याम् yuvābhyām | युष्माभिः yuṣmābhiḥ |
| Dative | तुभ्यम् tubhyam | युवाभ्याम् yuvābhyām | युष्मभ्यम् yuṣmabhyam |
| Ablative | त्वत् tvat | युवाभ्याम् yuvābhyām | युष्मत् yuṣmat |
| Genitive | तव tava | युवयोः yuvayoḥ | युष्माकम् yuṣmākam |
| Locative | त्वयि tvayi | युवयोः yuvayoḥ | युष्मासु yuṣmāsu |

The stems of aham and tvam derive from entirely different roots (the first person's m- stem versus the second person's tv-/yu- stem), reflecting the fact that personal pronouns already had their own inflectional system, independent of the noun paradigms, as far back as Proto-Indo-European.

## Enclitic (Unaccented) Short Forms

Alongside the full forms, the accusative, dative, and genitive singular and plural have separate **enclitic forms** — unaccented short forms used when the pronoun is not emphasized in the sentence.

| Case/Number | Full form (1st person) | Enclitic form | Full form (2nd person) | Enclitic form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accusative singular | mām | मा mā | tvām | त्वा tvā |
| Dative/genitive singular | mahyam / mama | मे me | tubhyam / tava | ते te |
| Accusative plural | asmān | नः naḥ | yuṣmān | वः vaḥ |
| Dative/genitive plural | asmabhyam / asmākam | नः naḥ | yuṣmabhyam / yuṣmākam | वः vaḥ |

Enclitic forms cannot stand at the beginning of a sentence — they are always prosodically dependent on the preceding word.

## The Demonstrative/Third-Person Pronoun tad "That/He/She/It"

Unlike aham and tvam, tad inflects for all three genders — masculine, feminine, and neuter — just like a noun. It is Sanskrit's only fully paradigmatic third-person pronoun; Sanskrit has no dedicated third-person pronoun of its own, so the demonstrative tad does double duty.

### Masculine

| Case | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | सः saḥ | तौ tau | ते te |
| Accusative | तम् tam | तौ tau | तान् tān |
| Instrumental | तेन tena | ताभ्याम् tābhyām | तैः taiḥ |
| Dative | तस्मै tasmai | ताभ्याम् tābhyām | तेभ्यः tebhyaḥ |
| Ablative | तस्मात् tasmāt | ताभ्याम् tābhyām | तेभ्यः tebhyaḥ |
| Genitive | तस्य tasya | तयोः tayoḥ | तेषाम् teṣām |
| Locative | तस्मिन् tasmin | तयोः tayoḥ | तेषु teṣu |

### Feminine

| Case | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | सा sā | ते te | ताः tāḥ |
| Accusative | ताम् tām | ते te | ताः tāḥ |
| Instrumental | तया tayā | ताभ्याम् tābhyām | ताभिः tābhiḥ |
| Dative | तस्यै tasyai | ताभ्याम् tābhyām | ताभ्यः tābhyaḥ |
| Ablative | तस्याः tasyāḥ | ताभ्याम् tābhyām | ताभ्यः tābhyaḥ |
| Genitive | तस्याः tasyāḥ | तयोः tayoḥ | तासाम् tāsām |
| Locative | तस्याम् tasyām | तयोः tayoḥ | तासु tāsu |

### Neuter

| Case | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | तत् tat | ते te | तानि tāni |
| Accusative | तत् tat | ते te | तानि tāni |

Beyond the accusative, the neuter forms match the masculine (tena, tasmai, tasmāt, tasya, tasmin, and so on).

## Key Differences From Noun Declension

- The masculine nominative singular saḥ and feminine sā are unpredictable from the stem ta- (this is an archaic Proto-Indo-European alternant, discussed below).
- The dative, ablative, and locative singular use distinctive endings — -smai, -smāt, -smin — not found in the a-stem noun paradigm. These so-called "pronominal endings" also extend to a handful of adjectives (sarva- "all," anya- "other," and the like).
- Apart from the masculine/neuter accusative plural, the dual and plural endings largely parallel those of the a-stem nouns (-au, -ābhyām, -ayoḥ, and so on).

## Example Sentences

```
सः तत् पुस्तकं मह्यं ददाति।
saḥ tat pustakaṃ mahyaṃ dadāti.
he-NOM that-ACC book-ACC I-DAT give-3SG.PRES
"He gives that book to me."
```

```
तस्याः पुत्रः त्वां पश्यति।
tasyāḥ putraḥ tvāṃ paśyati.
she-GEN son-NOM you-ACC see-3SG.PRES
"Her son sees you."
```
