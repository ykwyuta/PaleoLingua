## Basic verb structure

The Old Norse infinitive (dictionary form) typically ends in *-a* (e.g., *kalla* "to call," *tala* "to speak"). Verbs inflect for the person and number of the subject, and distinguish present from past tense. This article uses the most regular pattern, the weak verb conjugation, to sketch the basic skeleton.

## Present tense person endings

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | -a | -um |
| 2nd | -ar | -ið |
| 3rd | -ar | -a |

## Present tense of kalla ("to call")

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | kalla | köllum |
| 2nd | kallar | kallið |
| 3rd | kallar | kalla |

(The a→ö seen in the 1st person plural *köllum* is due to u-umlaut; see [Old Norse Phonology](/en/script-and-phonology/old-norse-phonology/).)

## Past tense of kalla ("to call")

Weak verbs form the past tense by inserting a dental suffix (one of *-ð-, -d-, -t-*) into the stem. For *kalla*, this is *-að-*.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | kallaða | kölluðum |
| 2nd | kallaðir | kölluðuð |
| 3rd | kallaði | kölluðu |

This dental-suffix past tense strategy is the direct ancestor of the English weak-verb past tense (*call → call**ed***). English *-ed* and Old Norse *-ð-* both descend from the same Proto-Germanic dental preterite suffix — grammatical cousins in the truest sense.

## Example sentences

```
ek  kalla    hana
ek  kall-a   hana
I(NOM) call-1SG.PRES her(ACC)
"I call her."
```

```
hann  kallaði   mik
hann  kalla-ði  mik
he(NOM) call-3SG.PAST me(ACC)
"He called me."
```
