## Why normalization is needed

Medieval manuscripts show a great deal of spelling variation from scribe to scribe, and the same word can appear in noticeably different forms across manuscripts. From the 19th century on, scholars — building largely on the description in the 13th-century Icelandic *First Grammatical Treatise* — established a **normalized Old Norse orthography** for convenient teaching and citation. Nearly all modern textbooks, dictionaries, and scholarly editions use this normalized spelling, and the grammar and reading articles on this site follow it as well.

## Consonants: þ and ð

Old Norse has two fricatives corresponding to English *th*, and normalized orthography distinguishes them with separate letters.

| Letter | Sound value | Position | Modern Icelandic reflex |
|---|---|---|---|
| þ (thorn) | voiceless dental fricative [θ] | word-initial | remains þ |
| ð (eth) | voiced dental fricative [ð] | medial/final | remains ð |

Example: *þú* ("you," initial þ) vs. *maðr* ("man," medial ð).

## Vowels: marking length

Old Norse is a language in which vowel length is contrastive. Normalized orthography marks long vowels with an **acute accent**, distinguishing them from their short counterparts.

| Short | Long | Short example | Long example |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | á | *jarl* ("earl") | *á* ("river") |
| e | é | *er* ("is") | *hér* ("here") |
| i | í | *í* ("in," preposition) | *ís* ("ice") |
| o | ó | *sonr* ("son") | *sól* ("sun") |
| u | ú | *upp* ("up") | *hús* ("house") |
| y | ý | *yfir* ("over") | *ý* ("yew") |

## Umlaut vowels

Old Norse also has a set of special vowel letters representing front-rounded and other vowels produced by umlaut.

| Letter | Approximate value | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| æ | long vowel near [ɛː] | i-umlaut of a |
| œ | long vowel near [øː] | i-umlaut of ó |
| ø | short vowel near [ø] (also written ǫ̈ in some editions) | i-umlaut of o |
| ǫ | short vowel near [ɔ] | u-umlaut of a |

Because i-umlaut and u-umlaut show up constantly in Old Norse noun and verb paradigms, these letters will recur often in the grammar articles.

## Other conventions

- Word-initial consonant clusters like *hl-, hn-, hr-* are kept as written, unlike in modern Icelandic (e.g., *hringr*, "ring").
- The glides /j/ and /w/ are written *j* and *v* respectively in normalized spelling (manuscripts often use *i* and *u* instead).
- Proper names and divine names are conventionally capitalized wherever they occur, not only at the start of a sentence (e.g., *Óðinn*, *Freyja*).
- Editions differ on whether to preserve the nominative *-r* ending in its fuller, older shape or to use a simplified form; this site follows the widely used simplified standard form.
