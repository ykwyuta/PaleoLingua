## Logograms

A logogram is a sign that represents a whole word rather than a syllable. Cuneiform began entirely as a logographic system, and much of Sumerian's core vocabulary is still written with a single logogram (or a small, fixed combination of them).

| Sign (transliteration) | Cuneiform | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `an` | 𒀭 | sky, god (as a proper name, the sky-god An) |
| `lugal` | 𒈗 | king |
| `gal` | 𒃲 | big, great |
| `kur` | 𒆳 | mountain, foreign land |
| `dingir` | 𒀭 | god, deity |

## What determinatives are

A determinative is a silent, unpronounced sign placed before or after a word to tell the reader what semantic category that word belongs to. In transliteration, determinatives are conventionally written in superscript.

| Determinative | Cuneiform | Position | Category | Example (cuneiform) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d` (dingir) | 𒀭 | before the noun | divine names | `d`nanna 𒀭𒋀𒆠 (the moon-god Nanna) |
| `ki` | 𒆠 | after the noun | place names | uruki 𒌷𒆠 (the city of Ur; uru "city" + ki) |
| `giš` | 𒄑 | before the noun | wooden objects | `giš`gu-za 𒄑𒄖𒍝 (chair) |
| `muš en` (muššen) | 𒄷 | after the noun | birds | tumušen 𒌅𒄷 (dove/pigeon) |
| `lu2` | 𒇽 | before the noun | occupations/groups of people | `lu2`simug 𒇽𒌣 (smith) |

## A note on reading

Because determinatives are silent, they are not pronounced aloud even though they remain part of the written form. `d`nanna 𒀭𒋀𒆠, for example, is read simply "Nanna," not "dingir-Nanna." It helps to think of a determinative as a scribe's own built-in label, added purely to prevent the reader from misidentifying the word.
